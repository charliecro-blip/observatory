## 2. The user journey, as it stands

**Minute 0–2 — onboarding.** Four intro slides teach the weather metaphor
well. Name → birth (city search fills coordinates and offset) → rhythm
(chronotype, wake/sleep, free windows). Every step skippable. Friction found:
none blocking. One trap: the birth **UTC offset pre-fills the standard-time
offset** — anyone born during daylight saving gets a chart one hour off
(≈ one house on the Ascendant) unless they correct the dropdown themselves.

**Minute 2–5 — first landing.** The Now page is genuinely alive on first
load: personal transits already in the rail, the correct planetary hour with
a plain-language prompt, three concrete Resonant Now suggestions, the calm
tide chart. Nothing demands setup. This is the right first impression.

**Minute 5–15 — the "aha" available.** Helm → New Guiding Star shows
"Supported by this season": their profection year, active chapters, and
exact personal transits, each one tap from becoming an anchored goal. This is
the app's most differentiated moment — no other product connects "Saturn is
crossing your 7th until 2028" to "so anchor this relationship goal to it."
Risk: nothing *points* a new user to Helm. The journey works if they wander;
there's no guided moment that says "now set your first star."

**Daily return (the loop that decides retention).** Morning glance at Now →
maybe check the day view in Ahead → do things → check off habits → evening
journal. Works, but: habit check-off is two taps deep (Helm → Habits), the
journal is at the bottom of a long Now page, and there are **no notifications**
— nothing calls them back at a transition ("Mars hour starts in 10 minutes,"
"the Moon goes void at 2pm"). Notifications are the single biggest retention
lever not yet built.

**A device caveat testers must be told:** there are no accounts. Identity is a
localStorage id; the birth chart lives server-side keyed to it. Clearing the
browser or switching devices = starting over. Fine for beta, must be said.

## 3. Simplicity vs. customizability — the working rule

The app already implicitly follows a three-layer rule. Making it explicit and
enforcing it on every future feature is the answer to the balance question:

- **Layer 1 — glance (zero config).** Now page + day view. One plain-language
  sentence answers "what kind of moment is this." Works with nothing but a
  timezone. Every word here must survive the "each and every word" test.
- **Layer 2 — steer (opt-in structure).** Guiding Stars, tasks, habits,
  lenses, chart styles, week/month views. Introduced by doing, not by
  settings: quick-adds, "Ride this →," a gear that appears where relevant.
- **Layer 3 — deepen (deliberate/premium).** Currents, the caution
  questionnaire, Launch, house systems, cycle tracking, the future Rhythm Lab.
  Behind a tab or a questionnaire the user chooses to take.

Rules that keep it honest: new features declare their layer before being
built; nothing in Layer 1 requires astrological vocabulary to act on;
configuration never lives in the main flow (gear menus and Settings only);
depth is always one tap away but never in the way.

## 4. What a user needs in everyday life

Four moments, and what serves them today:

1. **Morning glance (10 sec):** "what kind of day?" — Now hero does this well.
2. **Planning moment (2 min):** "when should I do X?" — lens best-times +
   day-view planetary hours do this; Launch handles the big-ticket version.
3. **Acting through the day:** hour prompts + habit check-offs — works but
   check-off should be closer to the surface (a habits row on Now would close
   this gap).
4. **Evening close (1 min):** felt-rating + journal — exists, buried, and
   localStorage-only. The retro loop ("was the app right about today?") is the
   trust engine and deserves promotion.

The connective tissue for all four is **notifications at transitions** — not
built. That's the roadmap's `§8` and should be the first post-beta build.
