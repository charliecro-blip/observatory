# Workshop: what do we call the thing? (tide vs weather vs …)

Owner 2026-07-23: "i want to workshop the tide language. any other way we can
speak about it? more weather, rather than tide?"

## What the word has to do

The hero concept names **the current quality of time** — its character (which
register is lit) × its level (how much charge). The word must: (1) be instantly
felt by a first-time user, (2) support *coherence not favorability* (a low is a
different mode, not a bad day), (3) give us a natural vocabulary for level,
change, and forecast, (4) not promise prediction.

## The candidates

### 1. Weather (the front-runner)
"Today's weather: bright and fast." / "A front of friction moves through late
afternoon." / "Heavy air till 2pm, then it clears."
- **For:** zero explanation needed — the app's own onboarding already says "a
  weather report for time," and Hand/Ebertin/George all reach for weather
  metaphors unprompted. Rich vocabulary: fronts, clearing, heat, pressure,
  bright spells, storms passing. Non-fatalistic by nature (nobody blames
  themselves for rain; everyone knows to bring a coat — *conditions, not fate*
  is already the report footer).
- **Against:** weather is something you *endure*; tide is something you *ride*.
  Weather has no built-in cycle — tides return, which carried the rhythm thesis.
  And "bad weather" creeps toward favorability language if we're not careful.

### 2. Tide (the incumbent)
- **For:** cyclical, rhythmic, sailable — the whole nautical register (helm,
  wake, log, compass) hangs off it. Level language (low/rising/high/ebb) is
  native. "Work with the tide" is agency language.
- **Against:** the owner's own fresh-eyes reaction — it needs the curve diagram
  explained; "Surge Tide · high, still rising" is a lot of proprietary
  vocabulary before breakfast. Tides are also *binary-feeling* (in/out) where
  days are textured.

### 3. Sky (plain descriptive)
"Today's sky: fire, building." — honest, minimal, but flat: no level, no
motion, no metaphor to steer by.

### 4. Current / Water (nautical-adjacent)
"The current is with you this morning." Strong agency ("swim with it"), but
we already use **Currents** for the long transits — collision.

### 5. Season-of-the-day / Light
Poetic ("the day's high noon comes at 3pm") but too abstract to instrument.

## Recommendation: weather as the LANGUAGE, tide as the INSTRUMENT

Don't replace — *stratify*. Weather is how the app **speaks**; the tide is how
the app **draws**.

- **Copy speaks weather everywhere** ("today runs hot and fast", "a patch of
  friction near 4pm", "clear water after the void lifts"). This is what the
  synthesis engine's woven flavour already sounds like — extend it.
- **The curve/level visual stays tidal** (the day-arc chart, low/high/ebb) —
  a *tide gauge inside the weather report*, for those who open it.
- **The four characters (Surge/Building/Clear/Deep) survive as-is** — they're
  element names, not tide names, and they work in both registers ("a surge
  day", "surge conditions").
- Hero header becomes weather-first: **"TODAY'S WEATHER — Surge · high"**
  instead of "Surge Tide · high, still rising" (one fewer proprietary noun
  before the user has context).
- Keep the nautical identity register (Compass, helm, log, wake) — it's brand,
  not mechanics.

**Cheap A/B before committing:** the intake already asks three questions — add
none; just flip the hero label copy behind a flag and watch the two friend
accounts + next testers. The deep rename (tide→weather in every string) is a
morning's work once the register is confirmed; doing it piecemeal now would
fork the vocabulary.

## Names actually used by the sources (for flavor mining)
Ebertin's book is literally titled "What Days *Favor* You" (we reject favor-
language); Hand speaks of "energies" and "life cycles"; Lewi says "conditions";
Hampar "the art of timing." Nobody owns "time's weather" — it's ours.
