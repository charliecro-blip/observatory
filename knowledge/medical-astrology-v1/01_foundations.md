# Part I — Foundations

The conceptual scaffolding the rest of this reference rests on. Before any planet, sign, or house can be meaningfully discussed in a medical-astrology frame, six things have to be in place: the symbolic stance, the four qualities, the four elements, the four humors, the four temperaments, and the logic of correspondence (sympathy, antipathy, rulership). After those, one final piece: what the tradition meant by "disease" and what Observatory tracks instead.

**Module feeds:** `Framework` · `Learn` · `Natal Blueprint` · `Body Weather` · `Workbench` · `Herbal Compass`

---

## 1.1 The symbolic frame

Medical astrology is not, and has never been, a theory of biological causation. It is a **symbolic language for noticing** — for naming the *shape, tempo, and quality* of what is happening in a body. The tradition's claims about planets "causing" disease are doctrine inherited from a pre-empirical period; the part of the tradition worth carrying forward is its **vocabulary**, not its mechanism.

Observatory's stance is consistent and absolute:

- The chart does not *cause* anything.
- The chart *describes resonances* — patterns the user may notice, name, and use to organize their attention.
- The body is the authority. Where the chart and the body disagree, the body wins. Always.

Every entry in this reference is to be read with that frame. Where a source says "Saturn causes melancholy" or "Mars produces fevers," what Observatory hears is: *the pattern called Saturn has the shape of melancholy in this tradition; the pattern called Mars has the shape of fever.* The pattern is what's being named. Whether the body is currently in that pattern is for the body to say.

This is not a hedge. It is the entire epistemological basis of the project.

## 1.2 The four qualities

The qualities are the smallest unit of the system. Everything else — elements, humors, temperaments, planetary natures, herbal properties, the doctrine of disease — is built from pairs of them.

There are four qualities, arranged in two pairs of opposites:

- **Hot** ↔ **Cold** — concerned with *activity, speed, heat, agitation, expansion* (hot) versus *slowness, cooling, contraction, sedation, rest* (cold).
- **Dry** ↔ **Wet** (also called Moist) — concerned with *form, definition, retention, hardness, separation* (dry) versus *flow, change, softness, blending, looseness* (wet).

Each pair is logically independent: a body can be hot-and-dry, hot-and-wet, cold-and-dry, or cold-and-wet. Those four combinations are the four elements (§1.3) and the four temperaments (§1.5).

The qualities are not metaphors. In the tradition's frame they describe *real states the body can be in* — and a great deal of medical astrology is the work of reading **which qualities are dominant, which are deficient, and which combination is currently expressed**.

### 1.2.1 The body's qualities are weather, not identity

A constitution describes a *baseline* mixture. The current state of the body is the constitution **plus** what the moment is adding to it. A naturally hot-dry person can be temporarily cold-wet (e.g. when sick with a cold). The tradition consistently distinguished:

- **Innate complexion** — the baseline, set by birth and slow to change
- **Acquired complexion** — the current state, set by season, diet, sleep, stress, activity, illness, age, mood

Observatory tracks both: the **Natal Blueprint** is the innate complexion; the **Body Weather** engine reads the acquired complexion as it shifts. Neither alone is the whole picture.

### 1.2.2 Degree

The tradition often graded qualities by **degree** — first, second, third, or fourth. A "warming herb in the first degree" is gently warming; "in the fourth degree" is intensely so (and often toxic at that strength). Observatory's Herbal Compass surfaces degree where the sources name it, with the same caution: high-degree material is for symbolic correspondence only, never dose.

## 1.3 The four elements

The elements are the four possible combinations of the two quality pairs:

| Element | Qualities | Direction | Season | Phase of life |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Fire** | Hot + Dry | Up / outward | Summer | Childhood / youth (in some sources) |
| **Air** | Hot + Wet | Outward / dispersive | Spring | Youth / early adulthood |
| **Water** | Cold + Wet | Down / inward | Winter (or autumn in some sources) | Middle age / older |
| **Earth** | Cold + Dry | Centering / downward | Autumn (or winter in some sources) | Old age |

The element of a sign, planet, or herb is shorthand for *which two qualities it carries together*. A Fire sign is hot-and-dry; a Water sign is cold-and-wet. This is why a single planetary entry can be summarized in one line ("Saturn is cold and dry, melancholic, of the Earth element") — those three pieces of information are mutually entailed.

> **Mixed testimony.** The element-to-season assignments vary across sources. Ptolemy gives one ordering; later medieval and renaissance sources sometimes shift water and earth between autumn and winter. Observatory preserves the *quality* logic (cold+wet = water; cold+dry = earth) and treats seasonal mapping as a regional/traditional choice the practitioner can name. The qualities themselves are not in dispute.

## 1.4 The four humors

The humoral system is the bridge between the cosmos and the body. The four humors are bodily *fluids* (in the tradition's anatomy) corresponding one-to-one with the four elements:

| Humor | Element | Qualities | Traditional organ / seat | Color |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Choler** (yellow bile) | Fire | Hot + Dry | Gallbladder, liver | Yellow |
| **Blood** (sanguis) | Air | Hot + Wet | Heart, arteries | Red |
| **Phlegm** | Water | Cold + Wet | Brain, lungs, stomach | Pale / clear |
| **Melancholy** (black bile) | Earth | Cold + Dry | Spleen | Dark / black |

⚠ **The humors are doctrine, not anatomy.** Choler, phlegm, and especially "black bile" do not correspond to substances modern physiology recognizes. The spleen does not produce black bile; there is no fluid in the body called melancholy. The humoral system is a **symbolic vocabulary** for talking about *qualities expressed bodily* — heat and inflammation (choler), volume and vitality (blood), congestion and softness (phlegm), structure and depletion (melancholy). Observatory uses humoral language as **pattern language**, never as physiological claim.

The tradition's catalogue of "humoral imbalances" — too much choler, deficient blood, etc. — is reframed in Observatory as **quality excesses and deficits** in the user's current weather. A user feeling overheated, irritable, and dry is having a *choleric pattern day*. The pattern is what's being named. Whether to act on it, and how, is the user's decision, possibly with practitioner support.

## 1.5 The four temperaments

The temperaments are the four humors as **constitutional types** — a person whose innate complexion runs to choler is *choleric*, and so on:

### 1.5.1 The four types

**Sanguine** — Hot + Wet · Blood · Air

Optimistic, social, expansive, warm. Tendency toward fullness — full blood, full appetite, full feeling. Heat dispersed easily through movement and engagement; moisture maintained through receptivity. Excesses run toward overheating, inflammation, plethora. Deficits run toward fatigue and flatness.

**Choleric** — Hot + Dry · Choler · Fire

Driven, sharp, focused, intense. Tendency toward fast metabolism, lean build, quick reactions, bright color, irritability under pressure. Excesses run toward overheat, agitation, anger, burnout, inflammatory patterns. Deficits run toward exhaustion of vital force.

**Melancholic** — Cold + Dry · Melancholy · Earth

Reflective, grounded, structural, slow. Tendency toward endurance, depth, conservation, gravity. Excesses run toward depletion, contraction, fear, isolation, chronicity. Deficits — rare in this type, but possible — show up as loss of form, lack of containment.

**Phlegmatic** — Cold + Wet · Phlegm · Water

Steady, soft, receptive, fluid. Tendency toward calm, retention, slow change, softness, accumulation. Excesses run toward stagnation, congestion, edema, dampness, lethargy. Deficits show up as dehydration or loss of softness.

### 1.5.2 No one is one temperament

Almost no one has a clean single-temperament constitution. The composite method (Part V) computes a *weighted profile* across all four qualities, and most people come out as a **primary with secondary**: sanguine-with-melancholic-undertow, choleric-with-phlegmatic-tempering, and so on.

Observatory takes this seriously: **a temperament label is only surfaced prominently when the chart is being clear** (the clarity-gating rule in §5.3). For most users, the more useful output is the *quality profile* — which quality is most concentrated, which is most scarce — not a label.

### 1.5.3 Temperament is not personality

The temperament/personality conflation is a real risk in modern astrology writing. Observatory keeps them distinct:

- **Temperament** is constitutional: it describes what the body tends toward — *tempo, qualities, what depletes it, what restores it.*
- **Personality** is psychological: how a person thinks, relates, communicates, feels.

The two correlate (a choleric person often has a sharp affect; a phlegmatic person often has a calm affect), but they are not the same thing. Observatory's temperament work is about **the body's weather**, not the user's character.

## 1.6 Sympathy, antipathy, and rulership

The doctrine of correspondences is the engine that lets the system *do anything* — without it, the planets and qualities just sit there. There are three core moves:

### 1.6.1 Rulership

A planet, sign, or other body **rules** something else when the two share a quality signature and a symbolic resonance. Saturn rules the bones because the bones are cold-and-dry, structural, slow to change, and bear weight — the *signature* of Saturn expressed in tissue. Mars rules the head (via Aries) because the head is forward, fast, and where heat rises.

Rulership is **descriptive**, not causal. Saturn does not run the skeleton; the skeleton expresses Saturn's signature.

In planetary medicine, rulership chains connect:

- A planet → a sign (domicile rulership)
- A sign → a part of the body (melothesia)
- A planet → a metal, herb, mineral, animal, place (correspondence rulership)
- A planet → a quality pair (innate temperament of the planet)

These chains let the system move smoothly from *which planet is active* to *which body part is implicated* to *which herbs are symbolically resonant*. The chains are not rigid; sources disagree (Mercury or Saturn for the right ear?), and the disagreements are preserved entry by entry.

### 1.6.2 Sympathy — treating by likeness

The principle that **like resonates with like.** A hot-dry pattern is supported by hot-dry foods, practices, plants, and timing — because those things *match the body's current quality* and amplify what it's already doing. Sympathy is the basis of *strengthening* approaches: feeding what is already present.

In the tradition, sympathy is often used when the body's current quality is *desired and underexpressed* — when a person needs more warmth, more grounding, more flow — and the supportive practice matches the goal.

### 1.6.3 Antipathy — treating by opposition

The principle that **opposite tempers opposite.** A hot-dry excess (overheating, inflammation, irritability) is tempered by cold-wet practices, foods, plants, and timing — because those things *balance* what's running too far. Antipathy is the basis of *cooling, opening, settling* approaches: counterweighting what is too concentrated.

The tradition's most famous antipathy logic shows up in Saturn-tempering, treated in detail at §2.1 (Saturn) and §V (cultivation): a Saturn excess is countered by Sun, Moon, and Jupiter qualities — warm, moist, expansive, generous.

### 1.6.4 Observatory holds both

Sympathy and antipathy are not in competition. They describe **two different moments** in the body's work:

- When a quality is *needed* and the moment is being asked to bring it forward → sympathy (feed what wants to grow).
- When a quality is *excessive* and the moment is being asked to settle it → antipathy (counterweight).

Different sources lean one way or the other (Culpeper is more sympathetic; many decumbiture writers are more antipathetic). Observatory carries both as legitimate styles. The practitioner and the user choose the orientation that matches the moment, ideally consciously.

⚠ All of this is described as **symbolic correspondence offered to the user**, never as treatment or prescription. The same plants, foods, and practices that the tradition lists as "treatments" appear in Observatory as **supportive practices** — things the user can try and listen for.

## 1.7 What "disease" meant in the tradition vs. what Observatory tracks

This section is the safety hinge of the entire reference. It cannot be skipped.

### 1.7.1 The traditional model

In classical, medieval, and early modern medical astrology, "disease" meant something different from what it means now. The closest translation is **imbalance of qualities** — a body that has drifted too far in one direction (too hot, too cold, too dry, too wet, or some combination), often along a particular humor. The tradition's catalogues of diseases under each planet ("Saturn's diseases: gout, consumption, palsy…") are catalogues of **named conditions whose quality-signature matches the planet** — not modern diagnostic categories.

The tradition's logic was: identify the imbalance → identify the planet whose signature matches it → identify the antipathetic planet → choose remedies under the antipathetic planet. This is internally coherent within the system but **bears no necessary relationship to what those named diseases actually are in modern medicine.** Gout, for instance, is now understood as uric-acid crystal deposition. The tradition's reading of gout as Saturnine is a *quality* reading (cold, hard, crystalline, slow), not a mechanism reading.

### 1.7.2 What Observatory tracks instead

Observatory does **not** track or surface named diseases. The app:

- Tracks **patterns** the user reports (energy level, mood, sleep, digestion, pain, mobility, etc.) in their own words
- Tracks **qualities** as the user describes them or as the engine infers from their reports (heat, coolness, dryness, moisture)
- Tracks **tempo and shape** of how patterns move (onset, duration, frequency, escalation, resolution)
- Surfaces **chart resonances** that match those patterns — *not* "you have a Saturn disease" but "the pattern you're describing has a Saturn-like shape"

The user is *never* told they have a condition. The app *never* names a diagnosis. The catalogue of "diseases under each planet" appears in this reference as **historical material**, with each named condition retired into the *quality and tempo* it represents.

### 1.7.3 The translation protocol

A consistent transformation applies wherever a source names a disease:

> **Traditional:** Saturn causes consumption (wasting).
> **Observatory:** Patterns that have a Saturnine shape — slow, depleting, structural — share a tempo with what the tradition called "wasting." Observatory describes the *tempo and theme*, not the condition.

> **Traditional:** Mars produces acute fevers.
> **Observatory:** Patterns that have a Martial shape — fast onset, hot, inflammatory — share a tempo with what the tradition called "acute fever." Observatory describes the *tempo and theme*, not the diagnosis.

The translation protocol is formalized in **Part X — Safety Floor**. Every entry in this reference applies it.

### 1.7.4 ⚠ Safety floor

- Observatory **never** names a medical condition based on chart data.
- Observatory **never** suggests the user "has" anything.
- Observatory **never** predicts disease, recovery, danger, or death.
- The historical disease catalogues in this reference are **for the engine's internal pattern-matching only**, with names retired into qualities and tempos before any user surface.
- When user-reported symptoms reach severity thresholds (chest pain, severe bleeding, sudden weakness, signs of psychosis, suicidal ideation, etc.), the app **escalates to "please see a clinician"** unconditionally, regardless of chart context.
