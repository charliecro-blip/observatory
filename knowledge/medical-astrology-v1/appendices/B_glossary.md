# Appendix B — Glossary

## Purpose

This glossary defines the technical terms used throughout the reference. Definitions are written for the **interested reader** — not for the working astrologer who already knows these terms (though they should be accurate enough not to mislead), and not for the absolute beginner (though they should be clear enough to bring someone along). Cross-references to the main reference entries are noted where the term gets deeper treatment.

---

## A

**Accidental dignity** — a planet's strength derived from non-sign factors such as house position (angular = strong; cadent = weak), aspects, speed, and sect agreement. Contrasted with **essential dignity** (sign-based). See Part IX (Workbench).

**Aldebaran, Antares, Regulus, Fomalhaut** — the "four royal stars" of Persia; fixed stars of significant traditional weight when conjunct natal planets. Not heavily used in Observatory's default reading.

**Almuten** — the planet of highest essential dignity at a specific degree, especially at the Ascendant degree (the "Almuten of the chart"). One of the eleven factors in the composite temperament calculation. See Part V.

**Angular** — describing the four cardinal houses (1st, 4th, 7th, 10th) and any planet placed in them. Angular placements carry maximum chart weight. See Part IV.

**Antipathy** — in the classical doctrine of plant correspondence, the principle that an opposite-register plant **tempers** a constitutional excess (e.g., warming plants for cold conditions, cooling for hot). Contrasted with **sympathy**. See Part VIII.

**Aphelion / perihelion** — the points in a planet's orbit when it is farthest from / closest to the Sun. Not generally used in interpretive astrology; relevant for some technical asteroid and outer-planet work.

**Apparent motion** — the perceived motion of a celestial body as observed from Earth, including retrograde phases. Most astrological work is based on apparent motion rather than true heliocentric motion.

**Applying aspect** — an aspect that is forming (the faster planet is moving toward exact aspect with the slower). Contrasted with **separating**. Applying aspects in horary and decumbiture are read as actively engaging the topic. See Part VII.

**Ascendant (ASC)** — the eastern horizon at the moment of the chart; the cusp of the 1st house; the chart's most heavily weighted single point in medical reading. The sign on the Ascendant is the **rising sign**. See Part IV, House 1.

**Ascendant ruler** — the planet ruling the sign on the Ascendant. The body's planetary significator in the natal chart. See Parts IV and V.

**Aspect** — the angular relationship between two planets (or between a planet and a chart point) measured in degrees of zodiacal longitude. Major aspects: conjunction (0°), opposition (180°), trine (120°), square (90°), sextile (60°). Minor aspects: semisextile (30°), semisquare (45°), sesquiquadrate (135°), quincunx (150°). See Part VI.

---

## B

**Benefic** — in classical doctrine, a planet whose nature is broadly supportive: Jupiter (greater benefic), Venus (lesser benefic), with the Sun and sometimes Moon also benefic in particular contexts. Observatory translates this language as **"supportive"** in user copy to avoid moral framing. Contrasted with **malefic** (Observatory: "demanding"). See Part II.

**Body weather** — Observatory's framing for the moment-to-moment effect of current sky on the body, with constitutional baseline informing how the weather is felt. See Part VI.

**Bound (Term)** — a subdivision of each sign assigning degrees to specific planetary rulers; one of the five essential dignities. The Egyptian terms are the most commonly used in traditional practice. See Part IX.

---

## C

**Cadent** — describing the four cadent houses (3rd, 6th, 9th, 12th) and any planet placed in them. Cadent placements carry less chart weight than angular or succedent positions; cadency is symbolically appropriate to the houses that hold less directly visible content. See Part IV.

**Cardinal** — describing the four cardinal signs (Aries, Cancer, Libra, Capricorn) which open the four seasons in the tropical zodiac. Cardinal-sign emphasis suggests initiative, beginnings, and the energetic register of opening. See Part III.

**Chaldean order** — the traditional speed-based order of the seven classical planets: Saturn (slowest) → Jupiter → Mars → Sun → Venus → Mercury → Moon (fastest). Used in the planetary day/hour calculation and in many traditional dignity schemes. See Part VI.

**Choleric** — one of the four classical temperaments; Hot + Dry; fire-element; yellow-bile humor in the classical anatomy. See Part V.

**Combust** — describing a planet within 8° (or sometimes 17°) of the Sun in longitude; classically a debility (the planet's expression is "burned" by the Sun's radiance). Different from **cazimi** (within ~17 minutes of the Sun; classically a strengthening).

**Composite temperament method** — the eleven-factor weighted calculation Observatory uses to derive a person's temperament from the chart. See Part V for full doctrine.

**Conjunction** — a 0° aspect; two planets at the same zodiacal longitude. The most powerful aspect type, blending the natures of the two bodies.

**Constitution** — the body's overall constitutional shape, including but broader than **temperament**. See Part V.

**Critical days** — in classical decumbiture, the days from illness onset when the Moon forms quarter-aspects to its decumbiture position (approximately days 7, 14, 21, 28). Classically read prognostically; Observatory uses them only as **reflection markers**, not prognostically. See Part VII.

**Culmination** — the moment when a planet (or chart point) reaches the Midheaven (MC). Highest visibility moment.

---

## D

**Debility** — the opposite of dignity; describing a planet placed in a sign or condition that weakens its expression. The classical debilities are **detriment**, **fall**, **peregrine**, and several accidental debilities. See Part IX.

**Decumbiture** — the practice of casting a chart for the moment a person takes to bed with an illness; the chart of that moment is read for symbolic insight into the illness. From Latin *decumbere*, "to lie down." See Part VII.

**Derived (turned) chart** — the traditional horary/decumbiture technique of reading one house of the chart as if it were the first of a derived topic. See Parts IV and IX.

**Descendant (DESC)** — the western horizon at the moment of the chart; the cusp of the 7th house; opposite the Ascendant.

**Detriment** — the sign opposite a planet's domicile, where the planet's expression is debilitated. Saturn is in detriment in Cancer and Leo (opposite its homes Capricorn and Aquarius). One of the major debilities.

**Diurnal** — describing a chart cast for a moment when the Sun is above the horizon (a "day chart"); also describing planets traditionally classified as diurnal (Sun, Jupiter, Saturn). See **sect**.

**Dignity** — a planet's strength or weakness derived from its sign and condition. **Essential dignity** is sign-based (domicile, exaltation, triplicity, term, face); **accidental dignity** is condition-based (house position, aspect, speed, sect). See Part IX.

**Direct motion** — apparent forward motion through the zodiac (opposite of **retrograde**).

**Domicile (Rulership)** — a planet's primary home-sign(s); the strongest essential dignity. Each classical planet rules one or two signs (Saturn → Capricorn, Aquarius; Jupiter → Sagittarius, Pisces; Mars → Aries, Scorpio; Venus → Taurus, Libra; Mercury → Gemini, Virgo; Sun → Leo; Moon → Cancer).

---

## E

**Eclipse** — a New Moon (solar eclipse) or Full Moon (lunar eclipse) occurring near the Moon's nodes, when Sun, Moon, and Earth align. Classically read prognostically; Observatory uses them as **emphatic weather** only. See Part VI.

**Ecliptic** — the apparent path of the Sun through the sky over the course of a year; the great circle the zodiac is measured along.

**Element** — one of the four classical elements: fire, earth, air, water. Each zodiac sign is assigned one element. Fire = Hot+Dry; Earth = Cold+Dry; Air = Hot+Wet; Water = Cold+Wet. See Part III.

**Equinox** — the two points in the year when day and night are approximately equal; the Sun crosses 0° Aries (spring equinox) and 0° Libra (autumn equinox). See Parts III and VI.

**Essential dignity** — a planet's strength derived from the sign it is in. Five layers: domicile, exaltation, triplicity, term, face. See Part IX.

**Exaltation** — one of the five essential dignities; a planet's sign of "elevated" expression. Sun exalted in Aries (19°); Moon in Taurus (3°); Mars in Capricorn (28°); Jupiter in Cancer (15°); Venus in Pisces (27°); Mercury in Virgo (15°); Saturn in Libra (21°).

---

## F

**Face (Decan)** — one of the five essential dignities; a 10° subdivision of each sign assigned to a planetary ruler (Chaldean order). The weakest essential dignity by point-weight.

**Fall** — the sign opposite a planet's exaltation, where the planet's expression is debilitated. Sun in fall in Libra (opposite exaltation in Aries), etc. One of the major debilities.

**Fixed** — describing the four fixed signs (Taurus, Leo, Scorpio, Aquarius) which sustain and consolidate the season they sit within. Fixed-sign emphasis suggests stability, durability, holding pattern. See Part III.

**Fixed stars** — stars whose position relative to the zodiac changes only very slowly (vs. the planets, which move rapidly). Specific fixed stars (Regulus, Aldebaran, Antares, Fomalhaut, Spica, Algol, etc.) carry traditional weight when conjunct natal planets. Not heavily used in Observatory's default reading.

---

## H

**Horary** — the branch of astrology that casts a chart for the moment a question is asked and reads it for the answer. Distinct from natal astrology (chart of birth) and from decumbiture (chart of illness onset, a specialized horary technique).

**Humor** — in the classical four-humor framework, one of the four bodily fluids (blood, yellow bile, black bile, phlegm) corresponding to the four temperaments. Observatory uses the qualitative framework without claiming the literal humor anatomy. See Part V.

---

## I

**Imum Coeli (IC)** — the bottom of the chart; the cusp of the 4th house; opposite the Midheaven. Latin: "bottom of the sky."

**Ingress** — a planet's entry into a new sign (or into a new house, for transits). Major outer-planet ingresses are significant body-weather events. See Part VI.

---

## J

**Jing** — in TCM, the body's essential reserve, especially associated with the Kidney system. Both inherited (pre-natal jing) and acquired. Cross-tradition note: parallels the Western "constitutional reserve" sometimes associated with Mercury via subtle constitutional reading, more clearly with the eighth house's depth-reserve register and with the fourth house's inheritance register. See Part XI.

**Jyotisha** — the Vedic (sidereal) astrological tradition of India. Anticipated as a future expansion in Observatory's cross-tradition framework.

---

## L

**Lord of the Geniture** — the planet of highest overall dignity in the chart; the chart's "ruling planet" in some traditions. One of the eleven factors in the composite temperament calculation. See Parts V and IX.

**Luminary** — the Sun or the Moon. The two luminaries are central in any chart reading.

**Lunation** — a New Moon or Full Moon. The lunar cycle's emphatic points.

---

## M

**Malefic** — in classical doctrine, a planet whose nature is broadly demanding or challenging: Saturn (greater malefic), Mars (lesser malefic). Observatory translates this language as **"demanding"** in user copy to avoid moral framing. Contrasted with **benefic**. See Part II.

**Materia medica** — the body of substances (plants, minerals, animal products) used in a medical tradition. The Herbal Compass surfaces the **plant materia medica** of the classical European tradition. See Part VIII.

**Melancholic** — one of the four classical temperaments; Cold + Dry; earth-element; black-bile humor in the classical anatomy. See Part V. **Distinct from clinical depression** (the modern mood disorder); the shared etymology is a translation hazard.

**Melothesia** — the head-to-toe assignment of body regions to the twelve signs (Aries = head; Pisces = feet; with each sign in between). The classical medical-astrology body map. See Part III.

**Midheaven (MC)** — the top of the chart; the cusp of the 10th house; opposite the IC. Latin: *medium coeli*, "middle of the sky."

**Modality** (or **quality**) — one of the three sign-modalities: cardinal, fixed, mutable. Independent of element. Each modality describes the energetic mode (initiating, sustaining, transitioning). See Part III.

**Modern outers** — Uranus (discovered 1781), Neptune (1846), Pluto (1930). Outside the classical seven-planet framework. Observatory holds them as **adjunct interpretive overlays**, not as primary dignity-bearing rulers. See Part II.

**Mutable** — describing the four mutable signs (Gemini, Virgo, Sagittarius, Pisces) which transition and close the seasons. Mutable-sign emphasis suggests adaptability, ranging, dispersing. See Part III.

---

## N

**Natal** — describing the chart cast for the moment and place of birth; the foundational chart for a person's constitutional reading.

**Nocturnal** — describing a chart cast for a moment when the Sun is below the horizon (a "night chart"); also describing planets traditionally classified as nocturnal (Moon, Venus, Mars). See **sect**.

---

## O

**Opposition** — a 180° aspect; two planets at opposite zodiacal longitudes. A polarizing aspect that highlights an axis. See Part III (axes).

**Orb** — the allowable margin of inexactness for an aspect. Different aspects and different traditions use different orbs. See Part VI.

**Oriental / Occidental** — describing a planet rising before the Sun (oriental) or after the Sun (occidental); a traditional accidental dignity factor.

---

## P

**Peregrine** — describing a planet with no essential dignity at all in a sign (no domicile, exaltation, triplicity, term, or face). Classically a debility; the planet "wanders" without anchor.

**Phlegmatic** — one of the four classical temperaments; Cold + Wet; water-element; phlegm humor in the classical anatomy. See Part V.

**Placidus** — a popular house system in 20th-century Western astrology, mathematically complex and based on time-arcs from horizon to meridian. Available in Observatory; not the default.

**Planetary day / hour** — the seven-day week and the planetary-hour subdivision, each ruled by one of the classical planets in Chaldean order. Used in cultivation strategies (Part V) and as body-weather signal (Part VI).

**Prakriti** — in Ayurveda, the constitutional type derived from the three doshas (vata, pitta, kapha). Parallels but does not isomorphically map to Western temperament or TCM constitutional types. See Part XI.

---

## Q

**Quadrant house systems** — house systems (Placidus, Regiomontanus, Koch, etc.) that divide the four quadrants of the sky unequally, producing unequal houses. Contrasted with **Whole-Sign** (signs = houses) and **Equal House** (12 equal 30° arcs).

---

## R

**Regiomontanus** — the house system used by Lilly, Saunders, and most of the 17th-century English tradition. Available in Observatory for practitioners working with classical decumbiture sources; not the default.

**Retrograde** — apparent backward motion of a planet through the zodiac, from Earth's perspective. See Part VI.

**Rising sign** — the sign on the Ascendant; the chart's primary constitutional anchor. See Part IV, House 1.

---

## S

**Sanguine** — one of the four classical temperaments; Hot + Wet; air-element; blood humor in the classical anatomy. See Part V.

**Sect** — the day/night distinction in classical astrology. **Diurnal sect**: Sun, Jupiter, Saturn; **nocturnal sect**: Moon, Venus, Mars; Mercury sect varies by position. A planet "of the sect of the chart" (diurnal planet in a day chart, nocturnal in a night chart) operates more harmoniously. See Part II.

**Semeiotica Uranica** — Nicholas Culpeper's 1651 medical-astrology manual, the most-cited single source in the reference. See Appendix A.

**Separating aspect** — an aspect that is dissipating (the faster planet has passed exact aspect with the slower and is moving away). Contrasted with **applying**.

**Sextile** — a 60° aspect; a supportive opportunity-bearing aspect.

**Significator** — a planet (or house) that stands for a person or topic in a chart reading. The Ascendant lord is the significator of the body; the 6th-house lord, of disease (in decumbiture); etc.

**Square** — a 90° aspect; a friction-bearing aspect that catalyzes through tension.

**Stationary** — describing a planet at the moment it appears to halt before changing direction (retrograde to direct, or direct to retrograde). Emphatic register. See Part VI.

**Succedent** — describing the four succedent houses (2nd, 5th, 8th, 11th); moderate strength, less than angular but more than cadent.

**Sympathy** — in the classical doctrine of plant correspondence, the principle that a same-register plant **supports** a constitutional pattern (e.g., Saturn plants for Saturn-prominent constitutions). Contrasted with **antipathy**. See Part VIII.

---

## T

**Temperament** — the body's fundamental qualitative composition (Hot/Wet/Cold/Dry; sanguine/choleric/melancholic/phlegmatic). See Part V.

**Term (Bound)** — see **Bound**.

**Tetrabiblos** — Ptolemy's foundational astrological work (c. 150 CE). See Appendix A.

**Tizhi** (体质) — in modern TCM, constitutional type; nine standard types in current TCM literature. See Part V cross-tradition note.

**Transit** — a current planetary position aspecting a natal point. See Part VI.

**Translation protocol** — Observatory's standard format for moving classical doctrine into user-facing copy with safety floor enforcement. See Part X.

**Trine** — a 120° aspect; a supportive harmonious aspect.

**Triplicity** — one of the five essential dignities; a planet rules the triplicity (element) of a sign by day or by night per the sect distinction.

**Tropical zodiac** — the Western zodiac measured from the spring equinox (0° Aries fixed at the equinox), used as Observatory's default. Contrasted with the **sidereal zodiac** used in Jyotisha.

---

## U

**Uranus** — modern outer planet, discovered 1781. Modern co-ruler of Aquarius. See Part II.

---

## V

**Void-of-course Moon** — the Moon between its last Ptolemaic aspect in a sign and its ingress into the next sign. Read as a "pause" weather. See Part VI.

---

## W

**Whole-Sign houses** — the oldest documented house system; each zodiacal sign is one house, beginning with the sign on the Ascendant. Observatory's default. See Part IV.

**Workbench** — Observatory's practitioner-facing synthesis surface. See Part IX.

**Wuxing** (五行) — the TCM Five Elements (Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, Water). See Part XI.

---

## Y

**Yang** (阳) — the active, expansive, hot, bright, solar, exterior pole of the TCM yin/yang polarity. Solar register. See Part XI.

**Yin** (阴) — the receptive, contractive, cool, dark, lunar, interior pole of the TCM yin/yang polarity. Lunar register. See Part XI.

---

## Z

**Zang-fu** — in TCM, the organ systems; **zang** (yin organs: Liver, Heart, Spleen, Lung, Kidney) store essence and qi, while **fu** (yang organs: Gallbladder, Small Intestine, Stomach, Large Intestine, Bladder, Triple Burner) transmit and transform. See Part XI.

**Zodiac** — the ring of twelve 30° sectors of the ecliptic. The tropical zodiac (Observatory default) measures from the spring equinox; the sidereal zodiac measures from a fixed star reference. See Part III.
