# Part VII — Decumbiture & Onset Charts

## What this part covers

This file documents the **decumbiture tradition** — the practice of casting a chart for the moment of taking-to-bed at illness onset, and reading that chart for symbolic insight into the illness. It covers:

1. What decumbiture is and where it comes from.
2. The classical decumbiture chart structure — the four-party frame (patient / disease / physician / medicine).
3. The Moon's motion during illness — the classical *critical days* doctrine.
4. **The Observatory exclusion** — the strict, permanent, non-negotiable exclusion of life/death prognosis from any module.
5. What Observatory's decumbiture module **does** offer: symbolic body-weather framing for an illness, supportive practice register, the body's request at the moment of taking-to-bed.

The reference documents the doctrine in full so practitioners can see the source material; the modules use only the safe registers.

## What decumbiture is

**Decumbiture** (from Latin *decumbere*, "to lie down") is the practice of casting an astrological chart for the **exact moment a person takes to bed with an illness** — the moment they are no longer able to continue with daily activity and must lie down. The chart of that moment is read for symbolic insight into the illness: its character, its likely course, its body-region, the practitioner-patient relationship, the medicine being applied, and (in the classical tradition) the prognosis.

The decumbiture tradition reached its fullest classical expression in the work of **Nicholas Culpeper** (*Semeiotica Uranica*, also known as *The Astrological Judgement of Diseases*, 1651) and **Richard Saunders** (*The Astrological Judgement and Practice of Physic*, 1677), drawing on earlier sources including Hippocrates, Galen, the Arabic medical-astrology tradition, and the broader European medical-astrology lineage. William Lilly (*Christian Astrology*, 1647) also addressed decumbiture within his broader horary practice.

The tradition assumes a worldview in which:
- Illness has symbolic structure that the chart can read.
- The timing of illness onset is meaningful (not arbitrary).
- The chart of that moment can guide practitioner reflection on the illness's nature and course.
- The Moon's motion during illness traces the disease's progression through "critical days."
- Specific chart readings can assess severity, prognosis, and (in older sources) survival.

Observatory adopts **the symbolic-structure layers** of this tradition and **rejects the prognostic and life/death layers** entirely.

## The classical decumbiture chart — the four-party frame

The decumbiture chart's most important structural feature is the **four-party frame**: four houses, each representing one role in the illness episode.

| House | Role | What it signifies |
|---|---|---|
| **1st** | The patient | The body of the person at the moment of decumbiture; the patient's vitality and immediate constitutional state. |
| **6th** | The disease | The illness itself — its symbolic character, its body-region affected, its planetary register. |
| **7th** | The physician | The practitioner attending the patient — the person treating, advising, holding. |
| **10th** | The medicine | The treatment being applied — the materia, the regimen, what the physician administers. |

The chart's interpretive work consists of reading these four houses, their rulers, and the **aspect dynamics between them**:

- **1st-6th** — patient and disease; how the body is meeting the illness.
- **1st-7th** — patient and physician; the practitioner-patient relationship.
- **1st-10th** — patient and medicine; whether the treatment is reaching the body.
- **6th-10th** — disease and medicine; whether the medicine is matching the disease.

The aspect-quality between the relevant lords (applying or separating, harmonious or stressful, perfecting in time or fading without completion) is read for the dynamics of the illness episode.

**Observatory's use of this frame.** The four-party structure is preserved as symbolic language for thinking about an illness episode. A user (or a practitioner working with a user) can use the frame to **reflect on the illness experience** — what is the body doing? what is the disease's character symbolically? who is the practitioner and what is the relational dynamic? what is the treatment and how is the body receiving it? This is reflective and supportive work. It is **not** diagnostic, not prognostic, and not treatment-recommending.

## The decumbiture chart's specific readings

The classical sources extract several specific readings from the decumbiture chart. Observatory preserves some, translates some, and excludes some.

### What Observatory preserves

**1. The disease's symbolic character.**
The 6th-house sign and ruler are read for the symbolic register of the disease. Aries on the 6th = hot, acute, head-region, Mars-flavored. Pisces on the 6th = fluid, dissolving, foot-and-lymphatic, Jupiter-flavored. This gives the practitioner and the patient a **symbolic vocabulary** for the illness — a way to talk about its register, its tempo, its felt character.

**2. The body-region affected (symbolic).**
The 6th-house sign suggests the symbolic body-region by sign-melothesia. This is read **symbolically** — as a register for reflection, not as a diagnosis of the affected anatomy. (The body itself, and the clinical assessment, are the actual sources of body-region knowledge.)

**3. The constitutional state of the patient at onset.**
The 1st-house ruler and any planets in the 1st are read for the patient's constitutional state at the moment of taking to bed. This is symbolic body-weather — how the body is showing up to meet the illness.

**4. The patient-practitioner dynamic.**
The 1st-7th aspect dynamic is read for how the practitioner-patient relationship is functioning. Useful for reflection on the care relationship; not used to evaluate the actual practitioner.

**5. The body's request.**
Drawing from the chart's planetary signatures and elemental balance at the moment of decumbiture, the chart can suggest what **supportive register** the body is asking for — warming, cooling, fluid, structural, restful, active. This is constitutional body-weather language adapted to the illness moment.

**6. The disease's tempo and phase.**
The Moon's position and the 6th-lord's condition give a sense of the disease's symbolic tempo — fast and acute, slow and chronic, with phase-changes visible in transit work over the course of the illness.

### What Observatory translates (heavily)

**1. The "kind of disease" attribution.**
Classical sources often name the disease type from the chart ("a hot dry fever," "a melancholic chronic complaint"). Observatory translates these into **symbolic register language** — *constitutional fire-and-dry register, acute tempo* — rather than as disease names. The translation discipline keeps the chart's reading symbolic and refuses the disease-naming move.

**2. The 7th-vs.-10th physician/medicine attribution.**
As documented in the houses entries, Observatory uses the seventh house for the physician-as-person and the tenth house for the medicine-as-treatment. The split resolves a classical doctrinal disagreement and gives each house a distinct role.

**3. The Moon's significations.**
The Moon's position, sign, phase, and motion during illness are read for the disease's tempo and the body's rhythmic relationship to the illness. The classical "Moon in the 8th house signifies death" doctrine and related Moon-prognostic readings are **excluded**; the Moon's symbolic tempo content is preserved.

### What Observatory excludes — strict, permanent, non-negotiable

**1. Life-or-death prognosis.**
The classical tradition contains extensive "signs of life or death" doctrine in decumbiture work. **Observatory excludes all of this.** No module reads any chart for survival prognosis. No setting overrides this. No practitioner mode unlocks it.

**2. Recovery timeline prediction.**
The classical tradition gives techniques for predicting recovery dates (often via Moon-transit to specific points). **Observatory does not use these.** Recovery happens at its own pace; the chart does not predict the timeline.

**3. Severity assessment.**
Classical sources read chart afflictions for severity grading. **Observatory does not.** Severity is a clinical assessment, not a chart-reading.

**4. The 8th-house Moon doctrine.**
The classical reading that the Moon in or transiting the 8th house at certain points signifies imminent death is **explicitly and absolutely excluded**.

**5. Crisis-day prediction.**
The classical critical-days doctrine (the 7th, 9th, 11th, 14th, 20th days from decumbiture as moments of crisis or resolution) is documented as historical material; Observatory does not use it as predictive content.

**6. The "judgment of the cure or kill"** language.
Some classical sources use this phrase explicitly. It is documented for historical completeness. Observatory does not engage in it.

## The Moon's motion during illness — the classical critical days

A specific traditional doctrine that deserves its own section, both for documentation and for clarity on exclusion.

### The doctrine

The classical decumbiture tradition holds that the **Moon's position and aspects during the illness episode trace the disease's progression**. Specific days are read as critical — moments of crisis, judgment, or potential resolution.

The most cited critical days from decumbiture:
- **7th day** — Moon has made a square to its decumbiture position (90° around the zodiac). First critical day.
- **14th day** — Moon has made an opposition (180°). Second critical day.
- **21st day** — Moon has made the second square (270°). Third critical day.
- **28th day** — Moon has returned to its decumbiture position (the **lunar return**). Fourth critical day; often read as the moment of resolution one way or the other.

Within these, sub-cycles based on the 4-day and 9-day arcs were sometimes layered. Saunders devotes several chapters to the critical-days doctrine and its prognostic content.

### Observatory's position on critical days

**The Moon's motion through the illness is real astronomical content.** The Moon does pass through quarter-aspects to its decumbiture position at roughly the days noted. Some practitioners (and some patients) find it meaningful to **track the Moon's phase relationship to onset** as a way of pacing reflection on the illness episode.

**What is excluded** is the prognostic reading of these days — the "if the patient is worse on the 7th day, expect crisis on the 14th" type doctrine. The days are documented as **symbolic markers** that some users may find useful for reflection; they are not used as prognostic content.

The translation discipline for critical-days copy:

> **Traditional:** The 7th day from decumbiture (the Moon's first square to its decumbiture position) is a critical day; the patient's condition that day signals the disease's course.
>
> **Observatory:** Approximately one week after illness onset, the Moon will form its first square to its decumbiture position. This is a natural reflection moment — a check-in point for noticing how the body's experience of the illness has evolved. The day is not predictive; the marker is a pacing aid, not a prognosis.

## What Observatory's Decumbiture module **does** offer

Restated positively, after all the exclusions:

**The Observatory Decumbiture module is a symbolic body-weather frame for an illness episode.** When a user enters the date, time, and location of an illness onset, the module:

1. Computes the decumbiture chart for that moment.
2. Reads the **constitutional body-weather** of the chart — the elemental balance, the planetary register, the Moon's position.
3. Identifies the **symbolic character** of the illness — the 6th-house sign and ruler giving a register vocabulary.
4. Frames the **body's request** at the moment of taking-to-bed — what supportive register the constitutional reading suggests.
5. Surfaces **reflection prompts** — the four-party frame as a way to think about the illness experience: how is the body meeting it? what is the disease's character symbolically? who is the practitioner and how is the relationship? what is the treatment and how is it being received?
6. Provides **Body Weather context** for the days following — how the lunar cycle and any active transits color the illness's evolving register, framed as reflection-marker rather than prognosis.

**What it does not do:**
- Diagnose. The chart does not name the clinical condition.
- Prognose. The chart does not predict outcome, timeline, severity, or survival.
- Prescribe. The chart does not recommend specific treatments, herbs, foods, or interventions.
- Override clinical care. The chart's reflection is symbolic; clinical assessment is medical.

## Onset charts more broadly

Beyond illness-onset specifically, the same logic applies to other meaningful onset moments:

- **First-symptom chart** — the moment a noticeable symptom first appeared, when illness was not yet acute enough to require taking-to-bed.
- **Treatment-start chart** — the moment a specific treatment was begun (a new medication, a new therapy, a course of work).
- **Hospitalization chart** — the moment of admission to a hospital or care facility.
- **Diagnosis chart** — the moment a diagnosis was received (a different layer from the illness onset itself).

Each of these can be read symbolically using the same framework: constitutional body-weather, the relevant houses and rulers, the four-party frame where applicable, the body's symbolic request. **All under the same safety floor:** symbolic only, never diagnostic, never prognostic, never prescriptive.

Observatory's Workbench module supports onset-chart work for practitioners; the user-facing Decumbiture module focuses on illness-onset specifically but can accommodate other onset moments through the same interface.

## The natal chart alongside the decumbiture chart

A subtler point: the decumbiture chart is most informative when read **alongside the natal chart**.

The natal chart provides the constitutional baseline. The decumbiture chart shows the symbolic body-weather at the moment of illness onset. Together, they describe **how this specific body's constitution is meeting this specific moment**.

A decumbiture chart with strong Mars emphasis on a constitutionally choleric body shows **constitution and weather in agreement** — the illness is operating in the constitution's native register, potentially intensifying it. A decumbiture chart with strong Saturn emphasis on a constitutionally sanguine body shows **constitution and weather in tension** — the illness is asking the body to operate in an unfamiliar register, which may explain why this episode feels particularly demanding.

Observatory's Decumbiture module reads natal-and-decumbiture together by default; the composite gives the richer reading.

## Cross-tradition note (TCM and illness onset)

TCM has no decumbiture tradition — its diagnostic frame works directly with the body's signs in the moment, not with the symbolic timing of illness onset. But both traditions share a principle: **the moment of illness onset carries information about the illness's character.**

In TCM, the practitioner pays close attention to:
- **What time of year** the illness arose (seasonal pathology — wind-cold in winter, summer-heat in summer, dampness in late summer).
- **What time of day** the symptoms are worst (mapped against the Organ Clock — symptoms worse at 3-5 AM suggest Lung; at 11 PM-1 AM, Gallbladder; etc.).
- **What recent activity or environmental exposure** preceded onset (carrying weight outdoors in wind = wind-cold invasion; eating cold food in damp weather = dampness with cold).

This is **empirical onset reading** rather than astrological-symbolic onset reading. Observatory and TCM use different methods to attend to the same principle.

**Where they converge:** both treat the moment of onset as carrying meaningful information for reflection on the illness. **Where they diverge:** TCM's onset reading is empirical and immediate; Western decumbiture is symbolic and structured by the chart. Both can be useful side by side.

## Safety flags ⚠

- **Life/death prognosis firewall — absolute.** Observatory does **not** assess survival, recovery prognosis, or "signs of life or death" from any chart, in any module, ever. This firewall is permanent, unconditional, and not subject to any practitioner-mode override.
- **Diagnosis firewall.** The decumbiture chart does **not** diagnose specific clinical conditions. The chart's 6th-house sign suggests symbolic register; it does not name diseases.
- **Prognosis firewall — strict.** The chart does not predict illness duration, severity, recovery timeline, or any specific outcome.
- **Treatment prescription firewall.** The chart does not recommend, contraindicate, or evaluate specific medical treatments. The 10th-house "medicine" attribution is symbolic register only.
- **Practitioner-evaluation firewall.** The chart's 7th-house "physician" attribution is symbolic; it is not used to evaluate or judge actual practitioners.
- **Critical-days reframing.** The classical critical-days doctrine is reframed as **reflection markers**, not prognostic crisis days.
- **Anxiety amplification — the highest-stakes safety zone.** People reading their decumbiture chart are by definition unwell. The safety floor is at its absolute strictest here. Every reading must support the body's autonomy and the medical system that is actually treating the body; nothing in the reading may amplify fear or substitute for clinical care.
- **Translation discipline — strict and constant.** Every classical reading that reaches a user-facing surface must be paired with the Observatory translation that strips the prognostic register. The doctrine "the 6th-house lord afflicted by Saturn in detriment signifies a long heavy chronic illness with poor prognosis" is **never** delivered; the translation "the 6th-house symbolic register suggests a slow, structural-tempo register for this illness; the body's request appears to be for patient, durable, supportive practice" is.
- **Decumbiture for someone else.** A practitioner or family member casting a decumbiture chart for someone else (a patient, a child, a loved one) is in a particularly sensitive role. The same safety floor applies; the reading is reflection only, not diagnosis or prognosis.
