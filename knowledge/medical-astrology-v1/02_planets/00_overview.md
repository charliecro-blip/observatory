# Part II — The Planets · Overview

The planets are the **acting agents** of the symbolic system. Where qualities, elements, and humors are static categories, the planets are *characters* — each with a temperament, a domain, a body of correspondences, and a recognizable signature when active.

This file frames the planetary system as a whole. Each planet then has its own file (`01_saturn.md` through `10_pluto.md`) containing the full layered entry.

**Module feeds:** `Framework` · `Natal Blueprint` · `Body Weather` · `Workbench` · `Herbal Compass` · `Decumbiture`

## 2.0.1 The seven visible planets

The classical system uses the seven bodies visible to the naked eye, in their **Chaldean order** (slowest to fastest):

| Planet | Symbol | Innate qualities | Humor | Temperament | Day of week |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Saturn** | ♄ | Cold + Dry | Melancholy | Melancholic | Saturday |
| **Jupiter** | ♃ | Hot + Wet | Blood | Sanguine | Thursday |
| **Mars** | ♂ | Hot + Dry | Choler | Choleric | Tuesday |
| **Sun** | ☉ | Hot + Dry (mod.) | Choler / vital heat | (its own — sometimes choleric) | Sunday |
| **Venus** | ♀ | Cool + Wet | Phlegm | Phlegmatic (mild) | Friday |
| **Mercury** | ☿ | Mutable (takes on whatever it touches) | Variable | Variable | Wednesday |
| **Moon** | ☽ | Cold + Wet | Phlegm | Phlegmatic | Monday |

A few notes on this grid:

- **Sun's qualities.** Most sources call the Sun hot and dry, but in **moderation** — its heat is the vital warmth of life rather than the parching heat of Mars. Some sources (Ficino especially) treat the Sun as a balanced source of warmth and light rather than as choleric.
- **Mercury's mutability.** Mercury takes on the qualities of whatever it most recently aspected or whatever sign it occupies. It is the only planet without a fixed temperament — which is itself a temperament (the *variable* one).
- **Day-of-week rulerships** form the basis of the planetary-day-and-hour cycle (Part V §5.4) and are used in the cultivation strategies.

### 2.0.2 The modern outers

The three planets discovered with telescopes (Uranus 1781, Neptune 1846, Pluto 1930) sit outside the classical seven-planet system. Observatory includes them as full entries because:

- They are part of the working vocabulary of every modern medical-astrology source we draw on (Cornell, Carter, Ridder-Patrick, Nauman, Hofman, etc.).
- They name *real signatures* in chart work that the classical seven cannot quite reach — particularly around nervous-system patterns (Uranus), dissolution and immune-system themes (Neptune), and deep, chronic, transformational patterns (Pluto).

But Observatory also flags two cautions:

- **The modern outers carry less consensus.** Sources disagree more about Uranus, Neptune, and Pluto than about the classical seven. Mixed testimony is more common; doctrine is less settled.
- **They are slow.** Uranus moves a sign every ~7 years; Neptune every ~14; Pluto every ~14–30 depending on sign. This means their natal placement is shared by everyone in a several-year cohort, and a strong individual reading requires *house position and aspect*, not sign alone. Body Weather output involving these planets is usually about *long-arc weather* (years), not daily/weekly weather.

The outer entries follow the same layered structure as the classical seven, with the additional layer **Modern doctrine vs. ancient analogues** — what (if anything) the older tradition called this signature before there was a planet to attach it to.

### 2.0.3 The luminaries

The Sun and Moon are called **luminaries** (lights), distinguished from the five other classical planets:

- **Sun** is the *vital principle* — the body's life force, vitality, animation, the heart, the eyes, the sense of self.
- **Moon** is the *receptive principle* — the body's fluids, rhythms, responsiveness, the stomach (in some sources), the breasts, the lived flesh.

Together the luminaries describe the **two faces of embodiment**: the active fire of being alive (Sun) and the receptive water of being a body that flows, holds, and feels (Moon). Almost every medical chart reading checks the luminaries first.

In TCM cross-tradition framing (Part XI), the luminaries correspond to **yang** (Sun) and **yin** (Moon) at a level above the five-element system. They are not part of *wuxing* but stand as the meta-categories underneath which the five elements operate.

### 2.0.4 The malefic / benefic frame, retired

The classical tradition divides the seven planets into:

- **Malefics** — Saturn (greater malefic), Mars (lesser malefic)
- **Benefics** — Jupiter (greater benefic), Venus (lesser benefic)
- **Neutral** — Sun, Moon, Mercury (taking on the quality of company)

This vocabulary is **retired** in Observatory's user-facing surfaces, for two reasons:

1. It is fatalistic. "Greater malefic" frames Saturn as inherently harmful, which is the opposite of how Observatory wants users to relate to Saturn weather (see §2.1 *When Saturn is a resource*).
2. It is misleading about how planets work. Even the worst-feeling Saturn weather is doing something *useful* in the body's slower work; even the best Jupiter weather can be overdone.

The malefic/benefic language is preserved in **Tradition** sections of the entries (because it is historically accurate to the sources), but never carried into Observatory translations or app copy. The replacement vocabulary:

- **Demanding** planets (Saturn, Mars, often the modern outers) — weather that asks more of the body, that requires conservation, attention, or active engagement
- **Supportive** planets (Jupiter, Venus) — weather that offers ease, expansion, restoration
- **Mediating** planets (Sun, Moon, Mercury) — weather that carries the qualities of what it touches

This is a real change in tone, not just word choice. App copy should never tell a user a transit is *bad*; it can tell them a transit is *demanding*, and offer the qualities that complement what it is asking.

### 2.0.5 Essential dignities and debilities

A planet's **condition** in a chart — how well or poorly it is "placed" — is read through the system of essential dignities:

- **Domicile** — the planet is in the sign it rules. Strong, "at home."
- **Exaltation** — the planet is in its sign of exaltation. Strong, "honored."
- **Detriment** — the planet is in the sign opposite its domicile. Weakened, "out of place."
- **Fall** — the planet is in the sign opposite its exaltation. Weakened, "demoted."
- **Triplicity, terms, faces** — finer-grained dignity categories (used in the Workbench, less so in user-facing surfaces).

The classical dignities, in summary:

| Planet | Domicile | Exaltation | Detriment | Fall |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Saturn | Capricorn, Aquarius | Libra | Cancer, Leo | Aries |
| Jupiter | Sagittarius, Pisces | Cancer | Gemini, Virgo | Capricorn |
| Mars | Aries, Scorpio | Capricorn | Libra, Taurus | Cancer |
| Sun | Leo | Aries | Aquarius | Libra |
| Venus | Taurus, Libra | Pisces | Scorpio, Aries | Virgo |
| Mercury | Gemini, Virgo | Virgo | Sagittarius, Pisces | Pisces |
| Moon | Cancer | Taurus | Capricorn | Scorpio |

In medical-astrology readings, the dignity of the **Ascendant ruler** and the **luminaries** is checked first; these describe the *condition of the body's vital principles*. A debilitated Ascendant ruler is read as a body whose primary signature is *less able to do its own work* (not "weakened" in a moral sense). A debilitated luminary is read as *vital fire or vital flow not running at full strength*.

⚠ The dignity scoring system can easily slide into deterministic readings ("debilitated Saturn → chronic illness"). Observatory's Workbench surfaces dignities as one input among many, never as a stand-alone verdict. Dignities describe *strength of expression*, not destiny.

### 2.0.6 Sect — diurnal and nocturnal

The **sect** doctrine assigns each planet to a "team":

- **Diurnal (day) sect** — Sun, Jupiter, Saturn
- **Nocturnal (night) sect** — Moon, Venus, Mars
- **Mercury** — sect by phase: morning-star Mercury runs diurnal; evening-star runs nocturnal

A planet is **of the sect** of the chart it's in (day chart vs. night chart). A planet in its own sect is gentler in its expression; one out of sect is more intense — Saturn in a day chart is more "cold limit," easier to work with; Saturn in a night chart is more "cold malefic," heavier-feeling. The same applies in reverse for Mars (gentler at night, harsher by day).

Sect is most useful in **Body Weather** for distinguishing how a Saturn transit (or Mars transit) is likely to *feel* — softer when in sect, sharper when out. The Workbench can surface this; user copy rarely benefits from naming sect directly.

### 2.0.7 Oriental and occidental

A planet is **oriental** when it rises before the Sun (visible in the morning sky) and **occidental** when it sets after the Sun (visible in the evening sky). The distinction shifts a planet's expression — oriental planets tend to be more *outward, active, expressive*; occidental planets more *inward, receptive, reflective*. The Haley composite sheet uses oriental/occidental as part of the planet's quality contribution.

In modern practice, oriental/occidental matters most for **Mercury and Venus** (since they're never far from the Sun), and is a useful additional descriptor for Mars, Jupiter, and Saturn in constitutional work.

### 2.0.8 Planetary speed and direction

A planet's apparent speed and direction (direct vs. retrograde) also modulate its expression:

- A planet at its **fastest** is most fully expressing its nature.
- A **slow or stationing** planet is concentrated, intense, sticky.
- A **retrograde** planet's expression turns inward — replaying, reviewing, holding rather than projecting.

In Body Weather, **stations** are noticed because the planet is "stuck" at a degree and its quality is amplified locally — a Saturn station near a natal Sun is a Saturn-tinted week or fortnight. Retrogrades of slow planets last months and read more as background weather than as event.

## 2.0.9 The entry template (a reminder)

Each planet file follows this structure:

1. **Tradition** — qualities, humor, temperament, body parts/systems, physiological signatures, traditional disease tendencies, herbal/mineral rulerships, therapeutic actions, symbolic role
2. **Mixed testimony** — where sources disagree
3. **Observatory translation** — the reframing
4. **When [planet] is a resource** — what the planet *gives* the body when working well
5. **Counterbalance** — how an excess is tempered
6. **Cross-tradition note** — TCM correspondence (where applicable)
7. **Module feeds** — Natal Blueprint, Body Weather, Workbench, Herbal Compass, Learn, Decumbiture
8. **⚠ Safety flags** — the planet-specific cautions
9. **Direct quotes worth preserving** — short citations from the sources
10. **Open questions raised by this entry** — collected to Appendix D

This template is the same one previewed in the outline document. Saturn (`01_saturn.md`) is the first full instance.

## 2.0.10 Reading order

The seven classical planets are filed Saturn → Moon (Chaldean order, slowest to fastest) because the medical tradition tends to read this way: the slow, structural planets first (constitutional weight), then the fast, modulating planets (current weather). The modern outers come last (8, 9, 10) and are read against the classical seven, not the other way around.
