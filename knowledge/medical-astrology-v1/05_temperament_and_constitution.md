## Temperament and constitution — the terms

**Temperament**, in classical Western medicine, is the body's **fundamental qualitative composition** — its native balance of the four primary qualities (Hot, Wet, Cold, Dry) and the four corresponding humors (sanguine, choleric, melancholic, phlegmatic). It is what the body is made of, in the doctrine's terms. Everyone has all four; the temperament names which one (or two) predominates.

**Constitution** is a slightly broader term — the body's overall constitutional shape, including temperament but also bodily structure, energetic patterning, vital capacity, and the specific resonances the chart shows. In ordinary practice, the two terms are often used interchangeably; for technical precision, Observatory uses **temperament** for the four-humor classification and **constitution** for the broader chart-derived shape that temperament is one (important) layer of.

Both terms describe **tendencies, not verdicts**. They name the body's leanings — what it tends to be more of, less of, more sensitive to, less sensitive to. They do not predict what will happen to the body, and they do not name a fixed identity.

## The four qualities and the four humors

### The four qualities (Hot, Wet, Cold, Dry)

These are the primary axes of the system. Each pair operates as an opposition:

- **Hot ↔ Cold** — the body's overall energetic warmth and activity register
- **Wet ↔ Dry** — the body's fluid register, density, and pliability

Every body has a balance along both axes. The four temperaments are simply the four possible combinations:

| Quality combination | Temperament | Humor | Element |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Hot + Wet** | Sanguine | Blood | Air |
| **Hot + Dry** | Choleric | Yellow bile | Fire |
| **Cold + Wet** | Phlegmatic | Phlegm | Water |
| **Cold + Dry** | Melancholic | Black bile | Earth |

This is the **temperament-element-humor lattice**. Every layer of the system — planets, signs, houses, herbs, conditions — can be mapped to these qualities, and any chart's temperament is its weighted sum across all the factors that contribute to it.

### What the humors are (and aren't)

Classical medicine treated the humors as **literal bodily fluids** (blood, yellow bile, black bile, phlegm). Modern medicine has obsolesced this anatomy — there is no "black bile" in any modern physiological frame. But the qualitative system — Hot/Wet/Cold/Dry, sanguine/choleric/melancholic/phlegmatic — works perfectly well as **a symbolic and qualitative language** about constitutional tendency, without requiring the literal humor anatomy.

Observatory uses the qualitative system. It does not claim that the body literally contains humors. The four-temperament framework is **how the chart's qualitative readings are aggregated** into a constitutional profile that practitioners and the body itself can recognize.

### The temperaments at a glance

- **Sanguine (Hot + Wet)** — air-element, blood-humor; broadly characterized as warm, moist, sociable, expressive, mobile; quick to respond, generous with energy, sometimes scattered or over-extended.
- **Choleric (Hot + Dry)** — fire-element, yellow-bile-humor; broadly characterized as warm, dry, driven, decisive, ambitious; capable of sustained heat, sometimes intense or burned-out.
- **Melancholic (Cold + Dry)** — earth-element, black-bile-humor; broadly characterized as cool, dry, deliberate, structural, durable; long-lasting capacity, sometimes heavy or depleted.
- **Phlegmatic (Cold + Wet)** — water-element, phlegm-humor; broadly characterized as cool, moist, receptive, adaptable, calm; deep reserves and steady tempo, sometimes sluggish or boundary-soft.

These are not personality types in the modern psychological sense (Myers-Briggs, Enneagram, etc.). They are **constitutional dispositions** — the body's qualitative tendency, expressed in physical, energetic, and (secondarily) temperamental-of-spirit terms. Observatory holds the constitutional reading primary and uses the personality language only carefully.

## The composite temperament method

The single most important technical content of this part. The classical tradition offers many ways to calculate a person's temperament from the chart; Observatory uses the **composite method** documented in Lee Lehman's work and refined in Dorian Greenbaum's *Temperament: Astrology's Forgotten Key* and in Joseph Crane's traditional-method synthesis (along with the specific eleven-factor matrix Observatory inherited from Charlie's *Haley_Temperament_Composite.xlsx* worksheet).

The composite method's principle: **no single chart factor determines temperament; the temperament emerges from the weighted sum of multiple factors.** This protects against over-reading any single placement and produces a more reliable result than older methods that named temperament from (say) the rising sign alone.

### The eleven factors and their weights

Observatory's composite calculation uses these eleven weighted factors, each scored as contributing to one or more of the four qualities (Hot, Wet, Cold, Dry):

| # | Factor | Weight | What it contributes |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Ascendant sign** | 4 | The rising sign's qualities (e.g., Aries → Hot+Dry; Cancer → Cold+Wet). Highest single weight. |
| 2 | **Other planets in 1st house** | 2 each | Each planet in the first house contributes its qualities. Multiple planets → multiple contributions. |
| 3 | **Ascendant ruler — sign quality** | 3 | The qualities of the sign the Ascendant ruler is in. |
| 4 | **Ascendant ruler — modality/phase** | 1 | Modal modifier (cardinal/fixed/mutable, or angular/succedent/cadent) of the Ascendant ruler. |
| 5 | **Planets aspecting Ascendant (within orb)** | 1 each | Each aspecting planet contributes its qualities, scaled by aspect. |
| 6 | **Moon sign** | 3 | The Moon's sign qualities (e.g., Moon in Scorpio → Cold+Wet). |
| 7 | **Moon phase / lunation register** | 2 | New, waxing, full, waning Moon contributes to Hot/Cold and Wet/Dry. Waxing Moon → Hot+Wet; waning → Cold+Dry; New → Hot+Wet; Full → Hot+Wet (with variants by source). |
| 8 | **Planets aspecting Moon (within orb)** | 1 each | Each aspecting planet contributes its qualities. |
| 9 | **Season of birth** | 2 | Spring → Hot+Wet (sanguine); Summer → Hot+Dry (choleric); Autumn → Cold+Dry (melancholic); Winter → Cold+Wet (phlegmatic). Northern hemisphere bias acknowledged. |
| 10 | **Lord of the Geniture** | 2 | The chart's highest-dignity planet contributes its native qualities. |
| 11 | **Almuten of the chart** | 2 | The planet of highest essential dignity at the Ascendant degree contributes its qualities. |

The total possible weight across all eleven factors is approximately **56 points**, distributed across the four qualities. The actual distribution depends on the chart.

### Worked example — the Haley chart

The Haley chart (Charlie's reference example, drawn from the *Haley_Temperament_Composite.xlsx* worksheet) totals:

| Quality | Score |
|---|---|
| Hot | 16 |
| Wet | 20 |
| Cold | 12 |
| Dry | 8 |

Reading the totals:
- **Hot + Wet = 36** (sanguine total)
- **Hot + Dry = 24** (choleric)
- **Cold + Wet = 32** (phlegmatic)
- **Cold + Dry = 20** (melancholic)

The dominant temperament for this chart is **sanguine** (Hot+Wet at 36, the highest combination total), with **phlegmatic** as a strong secondary (Cold+Wet at 32). The chart shows clear wet dominance (20 vs. 8 dry) and a moderate hot-over-cold tilt (16 vs. 12). The reading: **a strongly moist constitution with sanguine primary expression and phlegmatic substrate**.

### How Observatory's engine implements this

The Workbench module's temperament calculator:

1. Computes each of the eleven factors from the chart geometry.
2. Looks up each factor's qualitative contribution (using the planet-sign-quality tables documented in this reference).
3. Multiplies by the factor's weight.
4. Sums into a 4-row total (Hot, Wet, Cold, Dry).
5. Computes the four temperament-combination totals (sanguine, choleric, melancholic, phlegmatic).
6. Applies the **clarity-gating rule** (see below) before naming a result.

The user-facing Natal Blueprint shows the calculation's outputs in graded form. Workbench mode shows the full eleven-factor breakdown for the practitioner.

### Important calibrations

- **Weights are tradition-derived but adjustable.** The Haley/Greenbaum/Lehman synthesis is one credible set; other traditional practitioners weight differently. Observatory uses the documented set as default; the engine could in principle be reweighted if research warranted.
- **Season-of-birth assumes northern hemisphere.** This is the historical default and the assumption of the source texts. Users born in the southern hemisphere should have their season inverted (April in Buenos Aires = autumn, not spring). The engine handles this; the documentation notes the convention.
- **Phase modifiers and angular position.** Some traditional methods add modifiers for whether planets are angular, succedent, or cadent (with angular increasing the weight of the planet's contribution). Observatory's default does not apply this modifier in the temperament calculation (it would complicate the model without large gain), but the angular/cadent distinction is preserved elsewhere in the chart reading.
- **Moon phase scoring is the most variable factor.** Different sources give different qualitative contributions to the lunar phases. Observatory uses the Greenbaum-derived defaults; the engine could implement alternatives if warranted.

## The clarity-gating rule

**This rule is what protects the temperament reading from misuse.**

A composite calculation always produces a total; the totals always show one combination higher than the others. But the gap between the highest combination and the next-highest, and the gap between the highest single quality and its opposite, vary enormously across charts. Some charts produce a **clearly resolved** temperament (one combination dominant by a large margin); others produce a **mixed** result (two or three combinations close together, no clear dominance).

The clarity-gating rule:

> **Name a temperament only when the chart is being clear about it. When the chart is mixed, describe the qualitative tendencies without naming a temperament label.**

Why this matters: temperament labels are sticky. Once someone is told "you are sanguine," it tends to become an identity claim. When the chart actually shows clear sanguine dominance, this is useful and accurate. When the chart shows a mixed phlegmatic-sanguine result that could be read either way, naming "sanguine" overstates the chart's actual signal.

### How clarity is measured

Observatory's engine applies a **clarity threshold** with two checks:

1. **Combination gap.** The highest combination total must exceed the next-highest by at least **20%** of the highest total. (For the Haley chart: highest = 36 sanguine, next = 32 phlegmatic. Gap = 4. 20% of 36 = 7.2. The gap (4) is less than the threshold (7.2). Verdict: **not clearly resolved as sanguine.** The chart would be described as "predominantly wet, with mixed sanguine and phlegmatic expression," without naming one temperament as primary.)
2. **Single-quality gap.** The dominant quality on each axis (Hot vs. Cold; Wet vs. Dry) must exceed its opposite by at least **30%**. (For the Haley chart: Wet 20, Dry 8 — 60% gap, easily clears. Hot 16, Cold 12 — gap is 25%, just under the threshold.)

When both thresholds are cleared, the temperament is named. When one or both fail, the engine describes the qualitative tendencies without naming a label.

### What this looks like in practice

- **Clearly resolved chart**: "Your constitutional temperament reading is **choleric** — Hot and Dry, the fire-element register. The chart shows clear dominance of both heat and dryness, and Mars is prominent as the lord of the Ascendant and a contributing factor in the calculation."
- **Mixed chart (clarity-gating engaged)**: "Your constitutional reading shows mixed qualities — moderate heat and notable moisture — with both sanguine and phlegmatic expressions present in the chart. The temperament is not clearly resolved to a single dominant pattern; both registers contribute."
- **Edge case (one axis clear, one axis mixed)**: "Your constitutional reading shows clear moisture (Wet substantially over Dry) but mixed heat and cold. The wet register is reliable; the warm/cool register varies by context."

### Why this rule is central

Observatory's translation principle — "the chart never overrides the body; the body's lived evidence is primary" — applies sharply here. The temperament reading is constitutional language; it must be calibrated to **what the chart is actually saying**, not to what a categorical label would simplify to. The clarity-gating rule is the technical enforcement of that principle.

## Cultivation strategies

The classical tradition does not stop at naming a temperament; it goes on to **cultivation** — practices that work with the constitution to support balance and well-being. Observatory documents three main cultivation strategies, with their scope limits.

### 1. Humoral cultivation

The oldest cultivation logic: **like supports like; opposite tempers excess.**

- A **sanguine** (Hot+Wet) constitution is supported by moderate hot-wet practices (sociable warmth, generous nourishment, mobile activity), and **tempered** when in excess by cool-dry counterbalances (rest, structure, drier foods, moderation).
- A **choleric** (Hot+Dry) constitution is supported by purposeful hot-dry expression (challenging work, decisive action, lean nourishment), and tempered when in excess by cool-moist counterbalances (rest, ease, juicy foods, the Venus-Moon registers).
- A **melancholic** (Cold+Dry) constitution is supported by careful structural work (sustained tasks, mineral nourishment, durable rhythm), and tempered when in excess by warm-moist counterbalances (warmth, company, oily nourishment, sweetness).
- A **phlegmatic** (Cold+Wet) constitution is supported by gentle steady practice (water, rhythm, receptive work), and tempered when in excess by warm-dry counterbalances (movement, heat, structure, lighter and warming foods).

This is the framework the **Herbal Compass** uses to surface plant correspondences. A plant's qualities (Hot/Wet/Cold/Dry, plus its planetary rulership) are matched against the constitution's qualities under the sympathy/antipathy logic (Part VIII).

**Important scope limit.** Observatory **never prescribes specific foods, herbs, doses, or treatments**. The humoral framework supplies a **qualitative language** for thinking about cultivation; the actual choices belong to the person, in consultation with appropriate practitioners. The reference can name *registers* (warm-moist, cool-dry) and *categories* (sympathy / antipathy); it does not write prescriptions.

### 2. Planetary day and hour of birth

A subtler cultivation strategy from the classical tradition: the **planet that ruled the day and hour of birth** suggests a secondary planetary signature for the constitution, distinct from the chart's temperament reading.

The seven-day week has classical planetary rulership (Sunday → Sun; Monday → Moon; Tuesday → Mars; Wednesday → Mercury; Thursday → Jupiter; Friday → Venus; Saturday → Saturn). Within each day, the twelve daylight hours and twelve nighttime hours rotate through the seven planets in a fixed sequence (Chaldean order), with the first hour after sunrise ruled by the day's ruler.

A person born on **Saturday during a Saturn hour** carries a strong Saturn signature beyond what the chart itself shows. A person born on **Friday during a Venus hour** carries a strong Venus signature. The doctrine: this planetary signature is a **secondary cultivation key** — practices, environments, and qualities associated with that planet support the constitution at a level the chart's temperament doesn't always capture.

**Observatory's use of this:** the engine computes the planetary day and hour of birth and reports the planetary signature alongside the temperament reading. Cultivation suggestions are framed in terms of the planet's *register* (Saturn → slow steady work, structural support, mineral and earth registers; Venus → pleasure, beauty, social warmth; etc.) rather than as prescriptions.

**Calibration:** this is one input among several. A Saturn-day Saturn-hour person whose chart shows a clearly sanguine temperament is **sanguine with a Saturn signature**, not Saturnine — the chart's temperament is the dominant reading; the planetary day/hour is a secondary cultivation key.

### 3. Sign emphasis and dominant element

A simpler cultivation reading: when the chart shows **strong sign emphasis** in one element (multiple planets in fire signs, in earth signs, etc.) the cultivation register can follow the element.

- **Strong fire emphasis** — cultivation through movement, warmth, challenge, expressive practice.
- **Strong earth emphasis** — cultivation through routine, embodiment, mineral nourishment, structured work.
- **Strong air emphasis** — cultivation through conversation, learning, conceptual engagement, communicative practice.
- **Strong water emphasis** — cultivation through rest, rhythm, fluid environments, receptive practice.

This is a coarser reading than the composite temperament method but is useful when the chart's element emphasis is unusually pronounced.

## How constitution informs the lived body — and vice versa

A central principle, restated:

> **The chart describes constitutional tendency. The body's lived evidence describes what is actually happening.**
>
> When the chart and the body disagree, **the body is right.** The body's pulse, breath, energy, symptoms, and felt state are primary data. The chart is a frame for thinking about what those data mean — not a substitute for them.

This means in practice:

- A chart with strong choleric emphasis on a body that runs **cool and depleted** is a chart whose choleric register is **constitutional tendency**, not the body's current state. The cultivation work is to **warm and replenish** the body that is constitutionally choleric but currently cold — not to insist the chart-reading is right and the body wrong.
- A chart with strong phlegmatic emphasis on a body that runs **hot and dry** is similarly a constitutional tendency in mismatched expression. The cultivation work is to **support the body where it actually is**, with awareness that its native register is moister and cooler.
- A chart with mixed/unclear temperament reading on a body with **very clear lived constitutional signal** — the body wins. The chart describes the symbolic tendencies; if the lived constitution is unambiguous, the lived reading guides cultivation.

This principle is the **chart-vs-body firewall**, and it is enforced throughout the Natal Blueprint and Workbench modules.

## Cross-tradition note (TCM constitutional types)

TCM has its own constitutional typology — typically nine constitutional types (体质, *tizhi*) developed and codified in modern TCM literature. These are not isomorphic to the four Western temperaments, but the underlying principle (constitutional reading as primary, before condition assessment) is shared.

The nine TCM constitutional types include: balanced, qi-deficient, yang-deficient, yin-deficient, phlegm-damp, damp-heat, blood-stasis, qi-stagnation, and special-diathesis. Each carries a recognizable cluster of bodily signs and a corresponding cultivation strategy.

**Where the systems converge:**
- **Both** treat constitutional reading as foundational — not a label but a guide for ongoing cultivation.
- **Both** treat constitutional types as tendencies, not verdicts.
- **Both** prescribe cultivation in terms of *qualities* (warming/cooling, drying/moistening) and *categories* (foods to favor or moderate, practices to engage or limit) rather than as rigid prescriptions.
- **Both** acknowledge that constitutional type can shift over the lifespan and that current state may differ from constitutional baseline.

**Where they diverge:**
- TCM constitutional reading is **derived from clinical signs** (pulse, tongue, complexion, body shape, energy patterns); Western astrological temperament is derived **from the chart**.
- TCM's nine types do not map cleanly onto the four Western temperaments; the granularity is different, and the categories are organized along different axes (yin/yang, qi/blood/fluids, hot/cold, deficient/excessive).
- Some TCM types have rough Western parallels (yang-deficient ≈ phlegmatic-melancholic blend; yin-deficient ≈ choleric with dryness emphasis), but the mappings are loose.

**Practical convergence:** when a Western temperament reading and a TCM constitutional assessment agree, the convergence is informative. When they differ, both readings can be held simultaneously — the chart describes symbolic tendency; the TCM signs describe present lived state. The body itself is the arbiter.

## Safety flags ⚠

- **Temperament-as-personality firewall.** Temperament is a constitutional reading about Hot/Wet/Cold/Dry qualities, not a personality test or character verdict. Phrases like "sanguine people are extroverts" or "melancholics are depressed" are popular-astrology distortions; Observatory does not use them.
- **Mental health firewall.** The "melancholic" temperament shares an etymology with "melancholy" (the modern emotional state) and has surface resonance with clinical depression. **The two are not the same.** The melancholic temperament is a constitutional cold-and-dry register; clinical depression is a mood disorder requiring clinical care. The firewall applies to all four temperaments — none of them is grounds for inferring a mental health condition.
- **Treatment prescription firewall.** Cultivation strategies are framed in terms of qualitative registers and categories. **Observatory does not prescribe specific foods, herbs, doses, supplements, or treatments.** Constitutional reading informs reflection and conversation; clinical decisions belong to clinical care.
- **Constitutional identity firewall.** "You are sanguine" is shorthand; the longer truth is "your chart shows a sanguine constitutional tendency, which is one factor in how your body operates." The shorthand is acceptable in casual contexts; the engine prefers the longer form when it matters.
- **Chart-vs-body firewall (restated).** When the chart and the lived body disagree, the body wins. Constitutional reading is symbolic; lived constitution is empirical.
- **Body-typing caution.** Older sources sometimes describe physical appearance in detail by temperament (sanguine = "ruddy complexion"; melancholic = "thin and pale"). Observatory uses this very lightly — physical-appearance language can land badly across the diversity of bodies the app serves.
- **Cultural translation.** The four temperaments are a specifically Greco-Roman-medieval European framework. They are useful and have stood the test of practice, but they are not universal. The TCM cross-tradition note acknowledges that other systems carry equally robust constitutional readings, organized differently.
