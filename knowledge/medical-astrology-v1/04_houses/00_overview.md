# Part IV — The Houses

## Overview

The twelve houses are the **divisions of the local sky** as measured from a specific time and place — they are how astrology localizes the heavens to the body, the day, and the situation at hand. Where the planets are the actors and the signs are the registers, the houses are the **fields of life** in which the actors play their parts. In medical astrology specifically, the houses describe **where in the body, and where in the life-context of the body**, a planetary or zodiacal emphasis is expressed.

This overview file establishes:

1. What the houses are, in technical and interpretive terms.
2. Which house-system Observatory uses, and why.
3. The medical-house tradition — which houses are read medically and which are read incidentally.
4. The doctrinal stance Observatory adopts for the practitioner-facing surface (Workbench) and the user-facing surface (Body Weather, Natal Blueprint).
5. The link between houses and the body via the **derived/turned chart** logic in decumbiture.

The houses are the most technically contested layer of the whole framework. Observatory documents the contests rather than papering over them.

---

## What the houses are

A natal chart, decumbiture chart, or any horary chart is constructed by computing the planetary positions in the zodiac at a precise moment and place, then **dividing the sky relative to the eastern horizon** into twelve fields. The eastern horizon at the chart's moment is the **Ascendant** (the rising point); the meridian above is the **Midheaven** (MC); the western horizon is the **Descendant**; the meridian below is the **Imum Coeli** (IC, the "bottom of the sky"). These four cardinal angles are the chart's most powerful points.

The twelve houses follow from these angles. The first house begins at the Ascendant; the seventh begins at the Descendant; the tenth at the MC; the fourth at the IC. The remaining houses fill in between. **How** they fill in — the choice of mathematical division of the sky — is the question that produces multiple competing house systems.

**The houses describe fields of life.** Different astrological lineages assign different field-meanings to each house, but the broad consensus across two millennia of practice is approximately:

| House | Field |
|---|---|
| 1st | The body, vitality, the person themselves, the patient |
| 2nd | Resources, intake, what nourishes; in some readings, the throat |
| 3rd | Communication, immediate environment, short journeys, siblings |
| 4th | The home, ancestors, the foundation, what is buried; in some medical readings, the chest and lungs |
| 5th | Creativity, children, pleasure, the heart in some schemes |
| 6th | **Illness, work, daily routine, servants — the classical "house of sickness"** |
| 7th | Partnership, the practitioner, the other party, open enemies |
| 8th | Death, shared resources, transformation, inheritance — the classical "house of death" |
| 9th | Long journeys, philosophy, the higher mind, religion |
| 10th | Career, public role, authority, mother (in some traditions) |
| 11th | Friends, hopes, groups, the wider network |
| 12th | The hidden, confinement, self-undoing, hospitals, secret enemies |

The houses tagged in **bold** above are the historically medical-loaded houses; Observatory's medical-house treatment focuses on these.

## House systems — which one Observatory uses

Multiple mathematical systems for dividing the sky into twelve houses exist and have been used at various periods. The major contenders, with brief technical character:

- **Whole-Sign Houses** — the oldest documented system (used by Hellenistic astrologers and revived in modern traditional practice). Each sign is one house, beginning with the sign on the Ascendant. Simplest; works at all latitudes; does not require an exact birth time to function meaningfully.
- **Placidus** — the most common system in 20th-century Western astrology, particularly in popular and computer-generated charts. Mathematically complex; fails at extreme latitudes (above the Arctic Circle); produces unequal houses based on temporal divisions of diurnal arcs.
- **Regiomontanus** — the system used by Lilly, Saunders, and most of the 17th-century English tradition. Mathematically refined; common in horary and decumbiture work because the original sources used it.
- **Equal House** — divides the ecliptic into twelve equal 30° arcs from the Ascendant. Sometimes used as a compromise; doesn't reflect the actual sky's angular division.
- **Porphyry, Koch, Campanus, Topocentric, etc.** — minor systems with smaller followings.

### Observatory's choice

**Observatory uses Whole-Sign Houses as its default house system**, with **Placidus available as an alternative for users who learned with it** and **Regiomontanus available for the Workbench when working with traditional decumbiture sources.**

The rationale:

1. **Whole-sign is the oldest documented system** and is the system in which the medical and decumbiture doctrines were originally framed in the Hellenistic period.
2. **Whole-sign is robust at all latitudes** and to small birth-time uncertainties — important when many users do not have a precisely recorded birth time.
3. **Whole-sign keeps signs and houses aligned**, which makes the chart legible to learners (the third house starts at the third sign from the rising sign; cleaner pedagogy).
4. **Placidus availability** acknowledges that many modern users already work with Placidus charts and want their existing chart to render correctly.
5. **Regiomontanus availability for Workbench** allows practitioners to read decumbiture and horary charts in the original system the source texts were written in.

Observatory documents the difference and lets users see both when relevant. This is one of the doctrinal questions the reference does not pretend to settle.

## The medical houses

Six houses carry medical weight in the tradition. Observatory treats these at full depth in this part:

- **1st house** — the body itself, vitality, the patient. The rising sign and its ruler are the constitutional starting point.
- **2nd house** — what is taken in. Diet, intake, resources that sustain the body. In some readings the throat (though the throat is more typically Taurus-as-sign).
- **6th house** — the classical house of illness. Daily routine, work, the conditions under which the body labors. The traditional house of "servants" maps in modern reading to the relationships of daily-life dependency that affect the body.
- **7th house** — the practitioner, the other party, the relationship in which the body is treated or held. In decumbiture work, the seventh-house lord can signify the physician.
- **8th house** — the classical house of death. Observatory documents this house in full but **strictly excludes** any life/death prognosis from any module. The eighth house's symbolic content (transformation, shared resources, what is inherited, what is hidden in the body) is preserved without the fatalistic reading.
- **10th house** — career, public role, the conditions under which one is seen. In medical reading, the 10th-house lord can signify the physician (a doctrine in tension with the 7th-house assignment — sources differ).

The remaining six houses (3, 4, 5, 9, 11, 12) carry incidental medical weight in specific contexts but are treated more lightly in Observatory's reference. The fourth house has some medical relevance (chest/lungs in some schemes; the body's "foundation"); the twelfth has historical association with hospitals, hidden illness, and self-undoing patterns that the reference notes but treats carefully (the twelfth's traditional fatalism is one of the most adjusted-for layers in Observatory's translation work).

## How Observatory uses the houses across modules

**Natal Blueprint.** The first-house sign and its ruler are the **constitutional anchor**. Planets in the first house contribute directly to the body's constitutional register. The sixth house and its ruler are the **daily-conditions anchor** — how the body operates under routine. Planets in the sixth contribute to constitutional patterns of work, rhythm, and the body's everyday weather. The second house's intake themes contribute to nourishment-and-resource patterns.

**Body Weather.** When transiting planets cross house cusps, the activation moves into the new house's field. A transit entering the first house heightens body-and-vitality emphasis; entering the sixth heightens daily-routine-and-body-care emphasis. The transit's body-weather flavor combines the planet (which energy), the sign (which register), and the house (which field of life).

**Workbench.** The practitioner-facing synthesis surface uses house analysis as a primary organizing principle. The traditional medical chart reading — looking at the 1st, 6th, 7th, 8th, and 10th houses and their lords — is preserved in Observatory's practitioner mode, with the safety floor strictly enforced (no eighth-house life/death readings).

**Herbal Compass.** Houses are not directly material to plant correspondences (which are organized by planet and sign). House-based search is available as a Workbench extension — "show me plants resonant with the lord of the sixth in this chart" — but is not central.

**Decumbiture.** The decumbiture chart's first house is the patient at the moment of taking to bed. The sixth is the disease itself. The seventh is the practitioner or attending caregiver. The tenth is the medicine or treatment. The eighth, traditionally, is the outcome — Observatory documents the doctrine and excludes its prognostic use.

## The derived/turned chart logic

A technique used in traditional horary and decumbiture work: **turning the chart** so that another house becomes the first. The idea is that any specific topic can be read as if its house were the body's first house, and the houses derived from that turned position then describe the relevant fields.

For example, if the patient's first house is the body, the patient's sixth house is the disease. The disease, in turn, has its own "body" (the disease itself), so the seventh house from the sixth (the patient's twelfth) can be read as describing the disease's "partner" or the disease's "opposition" — what counters it.

This derived-chart logic is a powerful traditional technique. Observatory documents it in Part VII (Decumbiture) and Part IX (Workbench) as available to practitioners. It is **not surfaced in user-facing copy**, because the inferential layers stack quickly and the symbolic distance from lived body experience becomes large.

## The houses and the body — orientation

The twelve houses, like the twelve signs, can be read as a head-to-toe body map. This is a less established tradition than the sign-melothesia (which is the dominant medical-body map in the literature), but several lineages use it.

The most common house-body map runs:

| House | Body region (when read as a body map — secondary to sign melothesia) |
|---|---|
| 1st | Head, body as a whole |
| 2nd | Throat, neck |
| 3rd | Shoulders, arms, lungs (in some readings) |
| 4th | Chest, breasts |
| 5th | Heart, upper back |
| 6th | Abdomen, intestines |
| 7th | Kidneys, lumbar region |
| 8th | Genitourinary, eliminative |
| 9th | Hips, thighs, liver |
| 10th | Knees |
| 11th | Lower legs, ankles |
| 12th | Feet, lymphatic |

This house-body map is **isomorphic to the sign-melothesia** — the first house corresponds to Aries (head), the second to Taurus (throat), and so on through the twelfth to Pisces (feet). This is not coincidence; the houses inherit their body-region attributions from their corresponding signs in the natural zodiac (where Aries is the first sign, Taurus the second, etc.).

**Observatory's working practice:** the sign-melothesia is primary; the house-body map is secondary and used mostly in practitioner work where the two readings reinforce each other.

## The angles — special weight

The four angular houses (1, 4, 7, 10) and their cusps carry **special weight** in all astrological doctrine. A planet near an angle is highlighted; an angular planet's themes are more prominent in the chart. In medical reading:

- **Ascendant (1st cusp)** — the body itself, the constitutional anchor, vitality.
- **IC (4th cusp)** — the foundation, the chest and lungs in some schemes, the ancestors and inheritance.
- **Descendant (7th cusp)** — the practitioner, the other party, the relationship through which healing arrives.
- **MC (10th cusp)** — the public role, the medicine or treatment in decumbiture work.

When working with a chart, the angles are checked first. A planet exactly on the Ascendant carries constitutional weight that the same planet two houses away does not.

## Cadent, succedent, angular — house strength

Traditional doctrine groups the houses into three categories of strength:

- **Angular houses** (1, 4, 7, 10): strongest; planets here are most active in the chart's expression.
- **Succedent houses** (2, 5, 8, 11): moderate strength; the houses that "follow" the angles.
- **Cadent houses** (3, 6, 9, 12): weakest in classical scoring; the houses that "fall away" from the angles.

This strength gradation is used in dignity-and-debility calculations, in the Workbench's house-weight scoring, and in the temperament calculation (where house placement modifies a planet's contribution).

**Note on the sixth house's cadency.** The classical house of illness is itself a cadent house — one of the weaker positions in dignity scoring. The doctrine is that illness is associated with weakness of expression; the sixth-house position is symbolically appropriate to the field it describes. This is a teaching about how the tradition thinks about disease (as a falling-away from the angular vitality of the first house), not a verdict on people whose sixth-house lord is prominent.

## Doctrinal questions held open

The houses are the layer where Observatory has the most unresolved doctrine. The open questions include:

1. **House system default** — confirmed Whole-Sign with Placidus/Regiomontanus alternatives. Held open: is the user offered a choice on first chart calculation, or shown Whole-Sign by default with a "switch" option?
2. **Practitioner vs. physician — 7th vs. 10th house attribution.** Sources differ. Lilly and Culpeper lean toward the seventh; some Hellenistic sources to the tenth. Observatory's working practice: 7th in decumbiture, with 10th as the "medicine itself" (the treatment, the materia).
3. **The 12th house's translation work.** The fatalism here is heaviest — "self-undoing," "hidden enemies," "confinement," "hospitals," "secret illness." Observatory's translation requires the most adjustment for this house, and the 12th-house file documents the specific moves.
4. **The cadent-house strength gradation and the 6th-house teaching.** Should the doctrine be preserved in user copy (where it might read as "your illness placements are weak") or held to the Workbench (where the technical meaning is clear)? Default: practitioner-side only.
5. **Derived/turned chart logic.** Whether to surface this technique to advanced users via a Workbench toggle, or hold it as practitioner-only entirely.

These questions are collected in Appendix D for systematic decision when Charlie is ready.
