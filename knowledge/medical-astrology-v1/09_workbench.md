# Part IX — The Workbench (Practitioner Synthesis)

## What this part covers

The **Workbench** is Observatory's practitioner-facing synthesis surface. Where Natal Blueprint and Body Weather present chart content as accessible body-relevant register for the user, the Workbench presents **the full technical layer** — dignities and debilities, house analysis at depth, constitutional assessment with the eleven-factor breakdown, decumbiture work with the full classical doctrine (with permanent prognostic exclusions), the Herbal Compass at practitioner-detail, and the report-generation tooling that pulls all of these into a coherent synthesis for the practitioner-patient conversation.

## Who the Workbench is for

The Workbench is designed for **practitioners** — astrologers, herbalists, integrative health practitioners, somatic workers, and anyone whose practice integrates symbolic constitutional language with body-supportive work. It assumes:

- Familiarity with traditional astrological vocabulary (sign rulerships, house systems, aspect doctrine, dignity calculations).
- Working knowledge of the four-temperament / four-humor classical framework.
- A practitioner's discipline around scope of practice — knowing what the practitioner can do and what requires referral.
- A clear understanding that **Observatory's safety floor applies to practitioner mode as well as user mode**. The Workbench provides more technical content; it does not provide a path around the safety floor.

What the Workbench does **not** assume:
- That the practitioner is also a licensed medical provider. Many practitioners using the Workbench are not (and need not be) clinical providers.
- That the practitioner uses Whole-Sign houses specifically. Workbench supports Whole-Sign, Placidus, and Regiomontanus.
- That the practitioner agrees with every interpretive default. Workbench surfaces the doctrine and the available variations.

## The dignity-and-debility technical layer

The classical tradition has a precise scoring system for the **essential dignities and debilities** of each planet in any sign. This is one of the layers the Workbench surfaces in full.

### The five essential dignities

Each planet has, in each sign, a status determined by five overlapping dignities:

1. **Domicile (Rulership)** — the planet rules the sign (e.g., Saturn rules Capricorn and Aquarius; Jupiter rules Sagittarius and Pisces).
2. **Exaltation** — the planet is exalted in the sign, at a specific degree (e.g., Sun exalted at 19° Aries; Moon at 3° Taurus).
3. **Triplicity** — the planet rules the triplicity (element-group) of the sign, with different rulers for day-charts and night-charts (the classical sect system).
4. **Term (Bound)** — the planet rules a specific degree-range within the sign; the **Egyptian terms** are the most commonly used (also sometimes called bounds or limits).
5. **Face (Decan)** — the planet rules a 10° subdivision of the sign; the **Chaldean faces** are most commonly used.

These five dignities are scored on a points scale (often Lilly's: 5 for domicile, 4 for exaltation, 3 for triplicity, 2 for term, 1 for face) to give a total dignity score for the planet in that placement.

### The debilities

A planet can also be debilitated:
- **Detriment** — the planet is in the sign opposite to its domicile (e.g., Saturn in detriment in Cancer or Leo, opposite its homes).
- **Fall** — the planet is in the sign opposite to its exaltation (e.g., Sun in fall in Libra, opposite its exaltation in Aries).
- **Peregrine** — the planet has no essential dignity at all in the sign (no domicile, no exaltation, no triplicity, no term, no face).

Debilities are scored negatively (often -5 for detriment, -4 for fall, -5 for peregrine in some systems).

### What the dignity score tells you

The total essential dignity score is read as the planet's **functional strength in expression**:

- **Strongly dignified (+5 to +14)** — the planet operates near its native capacity; its themes express cleanly and reliably.
- **Moderately dignified (+1 to +4)** — the planet has some essential support; expression is functional but not at full capacity.
- **Peregrine (0)** — the planet has no essential support; expression is wandering, contextual, dependent on accidental dignities (house position, aspects, etc.).
- **Debilitated (-1 to -5)** — the planet operates against its native capacity; themes express with friction, indirection, or compensation.
- **Doubly or triply debilitated (-6 or worse)** — significant constitutional friction in the planet's themes; calls for careful, supportive work.

### Accidental dignities

Beyond the essential dignities (sign-based), planets have **accidental dignities** based on:
- **House position** — angular houses give strength; cadent houses give weakness.
- **Aspect to other planets** — supportive aspects to benefics strengthen; challenging aspects to malefics weaken.
- **Speed** — fast-moving planets are stronger; slow or stationary planets carry different weight; retrograde modifies expression.
- **Sect agreement** — sect-conforming placements (diurnal planets above the horizon in a day chart, etc.) are stronger.
- **Conjunctions to fixed stars** — specific stellar conjunctions add weight.

The Workbench surfaces both essential and accidental dignities in the planet's profile.

### What the Workbench does with dignities

The Workbench's dignity-and-debility view:

1. Shows each planet's essential dignity score in tabular form.
2. Shows the accidental dignity modifiers.
3. Identifies the **Lord of the Geniture** (the chart's overall most-dignified planet) and the **Almuten of the Ascendant** (the most-dignified planet at the Ascendant degree).
4. Highlights significantly debilitated placements for the practitioner's attention.
5. Provides the **constitutional reading layer** that flows from these dignities — e.g., a debilitated Mars suggests constitutional themes around drive, heat, and assertion that require more deliberate cultivation; a strongly dignified Saturn suggests the Saturn register is a constitutional resource the body can rely on.

**Critically:** the dignity layer is read for **constitutional register and supportive cultivation**, not for personality verdicts or character assessments. A debilitated Mercury is **not** "this person can't think clearly"; it is "this person's mental and communicative register operates with some constitutional friction; the cultivation work is to support what Mercury needs (clarity, articulation, the careful word) rather than to override it."

## House analysis at practitioner depth

The Workbench's house analysis goes deeper than the Natal Blueprint's surface:

### The full house-by-house sweep

For each of the twelve houses, the Workbench shows:
- The sign on the cusp.
- The house ruler (the planet ruling the sign on the cusp).
- The condition of the ruler (sign, house, essential dignity, accidental dignity).
- Any planets in the house, with their condition.
- Aspects from the chart's other significant points to the house cusp.
- The derived/turned chart implications (where relevant — see below).

This is read most heavily for the **medical-loaded houses (1, 2, 6, 7, 8, 10)**, with the cadent and other houses surfaced more lightly.

### Derived (turned) chart analysis

The classical horary technique of **turning the chart** — reading any house as if it were the first of a derived topic — is available in Workbench mode for the practitioner who wants to use it.

For example: if the user's natal sixth house (illness, daily conditions) is the relevant topic, the Workbench can show the **derived chart** with the sixth as the first — and then the sixth's "second house" (the patient-of-the-illness's resources), the sixth's "seventh" (the illness's partner — what stands across from it), and so on.

This is **technical content for practitioners** who are familiar with derived-chart logic. It is **not** surfaced to users in the main Natal Blueprint or Body Weather copy. The inferential layers stack quickly and the symbolic distance from lived body experience becomes significant; the Workbench is the appropriate surface for this depth.

### The four-party decumbiture frame

For decumbiture and onset charts specifically, the Workbench presents the full four-party frame (1st patient, 6th disease, 7th physician, 10th medicine) with the aspect dynamics between the relevant lords laid out for the practitioner to read. **The exclusions documented in Part VII apply absolutely** — no life/death, no prognosis, no severity grading.

## Constitution work — the full eleven-factor calculation

The Workbench's constitutional surface presents the eleven-factor composite temperament calculation (documented in Part V) **at full breakdown**:

- Each factor scored.
- Each factor's contribution to Hot, Wet, Cold, Dry shown.
- The aggregate scores by axis (Hot vs. Cold, Wet vs. Dry).
- The four temperament combinations (sanguine, choleric, melancholic, phlegmatic) totaled.
- The **clarity-gating** result — whether the temperament is clearly resolved or mixed.

The Workbench also surfaces:
- The **planetary day and hour of birth** (constitutional secondary signature).
- The **dominant element** by sign emphasis (count of planets in fire/earth/air/water).
- The **modality balance** (cardinal/fixed/mutable emphasis).
- The **sect** of the chart (day or night) and its implications.
- The **Lord of the Geniture** and **Almuten** as constitutional anchors.

A practitioner uses this full breakdown to **see the chart's constitutional structure** rather than just the summarized output. This supports the practitioner-patient conversation: the practitioner can show the user *why* the temperament reading is what it is, point to the contributing factors, and explain where the chart is being clear and where it is mixed.

### Cultivation register surface

For each constitutional reading, the Workbench surfaces the **cultivation register** — the qualitative registers (humoral cultivation), the planetary day/hour secondary signature, and the dominant-element register. These are presented as **vocabulary for the practitioner-patient conversation about supportive practice**, not as prescriptions.

## Decumbiture work at practitioner depth

For decumbiture and onset charts, the Workbench presents:

- The **full chart** for the moment of decumbiture (Whole-Sign, Placidus, or Regiomontanus per the practitioner's preference; Regiomontanus is the historical standard for English decumbiture work).
- The **four-party frame** (patient/disease/physician/medicine) with aspect dynamics.
- The **Moon's position and motion** during the illness — current sign, phase, void status, applying and separating aspects.
- The **critical-day reflection markers** — the Moon's quarter-aspects to its decumbiture position, framed as reflection points (not prognostic).
- The **constitutional baseline** read alongside the decumbiture chart (the natal chart's composite read informing how this body is meeting this moment).
- The **symbolic body-weather** — register, tempo, supportive practice register.

**The exclusions from Part VII apply absolutely:**
- No life/death assessment.
- No prognosis.
- No severity grading.
- No 8th-house death-doctrine reading.
- No critical-day crisis prediction.
- No "cure or kill" judgment.

The practitioner sees the full doctrine documented (so they understand what the classical sources said) and uses only the safe registers.

## Herbal Compass at practitioner detail

For practitioners interested in plant correspondence at depth, the Workbench surfaces the Herbal Compass with:

- **Full classical correspondence** for each plant (planetary ruler, sign, qualitative degree, body-region affinity, traditional virtues as Culpeper or other source recorded them).
- **TCM annotations** where the cross-tradition reading meaningfully informs the plant's correspondence.
- **Modern annotations** where modern clinical herbalist consensus differs significantly from classical attribution.
- **The full caution layer** — toxicity, pregnancy contraindication, drug interactions, intensity warnings, identification cautions.
- **Sympathy and antipathy register** for the user's constitutional reading or current body-weather signal.

**The scope limit from Part VIII applies absolutely:**
- No prescription.
- No dose recommendation.
- No treatment suggestion.
- All actual herbal decisions belong to the practitioner-and-user in consultation with qualified clinical herbalists or medical practitioners.

The Workbench's Compass is a **practitioner-grade reference** to the historical correspondence tradition with full safety-floor framing — useful for practitioners who want to think about plant register in their own work and who understand that correspondence ≠ prescription.

## Report generation

The Workbench's report-generation tooling pulls the layers together into shareable practitioner output. A practitioner can generate:

**1. Natal constitutional report.**
- Constitutional summary (temperament with clarity-gating, dominant element, sect).
- Eleven-factor breakdown.
- Planetary placement summary with dignities.
- House-by-house sweep for medical-loaded houses.
- Cultivation register suggestions.
- Cross-tradition notes (TCM constitutional cross-reference where relevant).

**2. Body Weather report.**
- Current sky for the user's location and time.
- Active transits to natal points (in-orb).
- Lunar position and phase.
- Planetary day/hour.
- Composite read combining constitutional baseline with current weather.

**3. Decumbiture / onset report.**
- The onset chart.
- Constitutional baseline.
- Symbolic disease register.
- Four-party frame.
- Body's request and supportive practice register.
- Reflection markers for the days following.
- **Strictly excluding** life/death, prognosis, severity.

**4. Workbench reading note.**
- A practitioner-authored note pulling specific elements from any of the above into a focused reading for a specific question or session. Authored by the practitioner (not auto-generated), with the system's content as input.

### Report format

Reports can be generated as:
- **In-app view** (shareable link, expiring per practitioner preference).
- **PDF export** (for printing or emailing).
- **Markdown export** (for the practitioner's own records or system integration).

All reports carry the **Observatory safety-floor framing** at the head and foot — the constitutional-not-clinical language, the chart-doesn't-cause-or-predict language, the body-overrides-chart language, the no-prescription language. This is non-removable.

## The Workbench-specific safety floor

The Workbench has more technical content than user-facing surfaces, but the **safety floor is not relaxed**. The opposite: because the technical content is richer, the safety discipline is **more actively enforced**.

### What the Workbench never does

Restated for clarity:

1. **No life/death assessment.** Decumbiture and any chart-derived prognostic content is excluded absolutely. No practitioner-mode setting overrides this.
2. **No clinical diagnosis.** The chart's symbolic register does not name medical conditions. Practitioners using the Workbench are responsible for staying in their scope of practice and referring as appropriate.
3. **No treatment prescription.** The Herbal Compass surfaces correspondence; it does not prescribe. The Workbench does not write treatment plans.
4. **No personality typing.** Constitutional reading is constitutional, not characterological.
5. **No fatalistic framing.** Even where the classical doctrine has fatalistic content (8th-house death, 12th-house self-undoing, etc.), the user-facing surfaces never deliver the fatalistic frame; the Workbench documents it for completeness with translation explicit.
6. **No medical authority claim.** Practitioners using the Workbench are practicing astrology, herbal correspondence, or somatic work — not medicine. The reports and tools are framed accordingly.

### Practitioner discipline

The Workbench assumes practitioners will:

- **Stay in scope of practice.** Astrologers astrologize; herbalists herb; medical providers practice medicine. Practitioners using the Workbench should be clear about which they are and refer appropriately.
- **Refer when needed.** The Workbench supports practitioners' reflection and conversation; it does not replace clinical care. Practitioners are responsible for recognizing when referral is needed.
- **Honor the chart-vs.-body rule.** When the chart and the body disagree, the body wins. Practitioners using the Workbench know this and frame their work accordingly.
- **Hold the safety floor for the user.** If a user is in distress, fear, or acute medical need, the practitioner is responsible for prioritizing the user's actual care over the symbolic reading.
- **Document the symbolic frame.** Notes and reports should be clearly framed as symbolic constitutional and timing language, not as medical guidance.

These are **practitioner-side disciplines** that the Workbench tooling supports but does not enforce; the practitioner's own training and judgment carry the responsibility.

## Cross-tradition note (clinical practice)

Practitioners working at the intersection of symbolic constitutional language and somatic/clinical practice come from many lineages — Western herbalism, traditional Chinese medicine, Ayurveda, integrative medicine, somatic experiencing, body-centered psychotherapy, and others. The Workbench is built primarily on the Western medical-astrology tradition but supports cross-tradition practitioners through:

- **TCM annotations** in the constitutional and herbal layers.
- **Future Ayurvedic integration** (held as a future expansion, not in initial scope).
- **The principle of holding multiple traditions side by side** without forcing isomorphism.

Practitioners are expected to bring their own clinical traditions; the Workbench supports their symbolic-constitutional layer without claiming to replace clinical knowledge.

## Safety flags ⚠

- **The Workbench is technical depth, not safety-floor relaxation.** The richer content is paired with stricter discipline, not weaker.
- **Practitioner-mode is not a path around exclusions.** Life/death prognosis is excluded everywhere, in every mode, permanently.
- **Practitioner-authored reports inherit the system's safety framing.** The non-removable framing at report head and foot keeps the constitutional-not-clinical, no-prediction, no-prescription, body-overrides-chart frame visible.
- **Practitioner scope of practice.** The Workbench supports practitioners in their own scope; it does not extend their scope. An astrologer doing Workbench work is still an astrologer, not a medical provider.
- **User-facing translation discipline still applies.** When practitioners share Workbench-generated content with users, the same translation discipline applies — symbolic register, not clinical naming.
- **No medical-authority impression.** The Workbench's depth could be mistaken for medical authority by an inattentive user. Visual and textual framing keeps the distinction clear.
- **Cross-tradition responsibility.** Practitioners bringing in cross-tradition content (TCM, Ayurveda, etc.) are responsible for their own training in those traditions; the Workbench's annotations are reference, not training.
