# Worked Examples

Three elections, walked through the way the "Launch" feature's output should
eventually read — reasoning shown, not a bare score (per `01_universal_rules.md`'s
closing note).

## 1. Launching a small business's website

**Category:** Business launch — 10th house, Sun/Saturn co-significators.

- Check Moon: not void, not in via combusta, not in her last degree — clear.
- Check Moon's last perfected aspect: separating from a trine to Jupiter (soft,
  supportive residue) — favorable.
- Check Moon's first applying aspect: a sextile to Venus, 6 hours out — a
  benefic applying aspect, strong support.
- Check Moon phase: waxing gibbous — favors growth. Good.
- Check Mercury: direct, not retrograde — clear (this category treats
  Mercury-retrograde as a soft caution, not hard, since it's not primarily a
  contract-signing election).
- Check the 10th house ruler (say Saturn, if Capricorn is on the 10th): direct,
  not combust, in a supportive aspect to the Sun — favorable.
- Check malefic affliction to the ascendant: none within 3° — clear.
- **Read:** a strong window. Refine to the hour: a Sun or Jupiter hour that
  day would sharpen it further — the scan should surface the specific hour
  block, not just the date.

## 2. Signing a publishing contract

**Category:** Contract — 7th (the other party) + 3rd (the document) houses,
Mercury as primary significator.

- Mercury retrograde: **this is the hard-block category** — if Mercury is
  retrograde, the feature should say so plainly and decline to call any date
  in the window "good," even if the Moon looks clean. This is the one place
  where a single rule should override the rest of the checklist.
- If Mercury is direct: check Mercury's condition (dignified? afflicted by
  Mars/Saturn?) and the Moon exactly as in example 1.
- **Read (Mercury retrograde case):** "Mercury is retrograde through [date] —
  classically avoided for signing agreements regardless of the Moon's
  condition. The nearest genuinely clear window is [date after Mercury turns
  direct + Moon clears void/via combusta]." This is a case where honesty about
  a bad answer (wait) is more valuable than manufacturing a good one nearby.

## 3. A new daily meditation habit (6th house, ordinary — not medical)

**Category:** Habit/practice start — 6th house, no strict co-significator
required (this is a low-stakes, ordinary election, not one of the
tradition's high-scrutiny categories like marriage or contracts).

- Because this is low-stakes, apply universal rules only, lightly: prefer not
  void, prefer waxing or at least not in the extreme final degree of a sign.
- **Read:** low-stakes elections shouldn't produce the same dense checklist as
  example 1 or 2 — the feature's tone should scale with the category's
  classical weight. A meditation habit gets one or two lines ("today's fine —
  Moon's clear and waxing"), not a seven-point audit. This maps directly onto
  the app's existing "simple by default, depth on demand" principle: the
  checklist *exists* underneath for every category, but the UI should only
  surface as much of it as the category's stakes warrant.
